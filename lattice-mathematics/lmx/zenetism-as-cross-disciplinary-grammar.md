\le C_m(\nu r)^{-1/2}\|F\|_{H^m},
\qquad
\|\mathbf u\otimes\mathbf v\|_{H^m}
\le C_m\|\mathbf u\|_{H^m}\|\mathbf v\|_{H^m},
\]

with constants absorbed into a single enlarged \(C_m\) in the following estimates. The first bound follows from boundedness of the Fourier projection and \(\sup_{k\ge0} k e^{-\nu r k^2}\le C(\nu r)^{-1/2}\); the second follows because \(H^m\) is an algebra for \(m>3/2\) in three dimensions. Integrating \((t-s)^{-1/2}\) yields

\[
\|\mathcal T(\mathbf u)\|_{X_T}
\le U_0+2C_m\sqrt{T/\nu}\|\mathbf u\|_{X_T}^2,
\]

\[
\|\mathcal T(\mathbf u)-\mathcal T(\mathbf v)\|_{X_T}
\le2C_m\sqrt{T/\nu}
(\|\mathbf u\|_{X_T}+\|\mathbf v\|_{X_T})
\|\mathbf u-\mathbf v\|_{X_T}.
\]

On the closed ball \(\mathbb B=\{\mathbf u\mid\|\mathbf u\|_{X_T}\le2U_0\}\), choose

\[
\sqrt{T/\nu}\le\frac1{16C_mU_0},
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad
T\le\frac{\nu}{256C_m^2U_0^2}.
\]

Then \(\mathcal T\) maps \(\mathbb B\) into the ball of radius \(3U_0/2\) and has Lipschitz constant at most \(1/2\). The physical retained-state weight is denoted by ρ to distinguish it from the corpus's separately defined contraction-gap quantities. For any fixed \(0\le\rho<1\), the relaxed map

\[
\mathcal T_\rho(\mathbf u)=\rho\mathbf u+(1-\rho)\mathcal T(\mathbf u)
\]

preserves \(\mathbb B\) and has contraction factor

\[
q_\rho\le\rho+(1-\rho)/2=(1+\rho)/2<1.
\]

Starting with \(\mathbf u^{(0)}(t)=e^{\nu t\Delta}\mathbf u_0\), successive iterates are divergence-free, retain the initial condition, and converge in \(X_T\) to the unique fixed point \(\mathbf u_*\) in the ball. A quantitative error bound is

\[
\|\mathbf u^{(n)}-\mathbf u_*\|_{X_T}
\le\frac{q_\rho^n}{1-q_\rho}
\|\mathbf u^{(1)}-\mathbf u^{(0)}\|_{X_T}.
\]

The limit solves the projected unforced momentum equation. Recovering

\[
p_*=-\Delta^{-1}\partial_i\partial_j(u_{*,i}u_{*,j})
\]

completes pressure compatibility. The solution is a strong local solution; for the smooth Gaussian initial data, parabolic regularity and the smooth-data local theory give smoothness on the short interval, with the prescribed initial trace. It has finite energy and positive viscosity. The nonlinear iterates generally leave the four-parameter family, exactly as the obstruction requires.

For the Gaussian initial field, the first heat iterate is explicit:

\[

\[
A(t)=A_0(R_0^2/R(t)^2)^2(Z_0^2/Z(t)^2)^{3/2},

### B.5 Exact Viscous Strain–Vortex Family

The native account requires motion to be assessed through its coupled function. A classical exact fluid realization of coupled radial contraction, axial extension, rotation, and diffusion is the Burgers vortex class. Its established standing, including imposed time-dependent linear strain, is explicit in Maekawa, Miura, and Prange, [On stability of blow-up solutions of the Burgers vortex type](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.10341), §1. The following parameter calculation is supplied directly for this application.

Let \(a(t)\) be smooth, let circulation \(\Gamma_v\in\mathbb R\) be constant, and take \(Q(0)=Q_0>0\). Define

\[
u_r=-a(t)r,\qquad u_z=2a(t)z,\qquad
u_\theta=\frac{\Gamma_v}{2\pi r}(1-e^{-r^2/Q(t)}).
\]

The apparent singularity at \(r=0\) is removable:

\[
u_\theta=\frac{\Gamma_v}{2\pi Q}r+O(r^3),
\qquad
\boldsymbol\omega=\left(0,0,\frac{\Gamma_v}{\pi Q}e^{-r^2/Q}\right).
\]

For nonzero circulation, substituting into the azimuthal momentum equation gives exactly

\[
\boxed{\dot Q=4\nu-2aQ.}
\]

For zero circulation, the velocity does not depend on Q; imposing the same width equation remains a consistent parameter convention. With \(I(t)=\int_0^t a(s)ds\),

\[
Q(t)=e^{-2I(t)}\left[Q_0+4\nu\int_0^t e^{2I(s)}ds\right]>0.
\]

For constant \(a>0\), the stationary core width is \(Q_*=2\nu/a\); deviations decay as \(e^{-2at}\). The corresponding pressure is

\[
p(r,z,t)=\frac{\dot a-a^2}{2}r^2
+\int_0^r\frac{u_\theta(\rho,t)^2}{\rho}\,d\rho
-(\dot a+2a^2)z^2+p_0(t).
\]

The integral is smooth at the axis. The radial and axial momentum residuals vanish with this pressure, and the azimuthal residual vanishes with the width equation. Unlike the affine field of Appendix A, the swirl has a nonzero viscous term. This is an exact unforced solution for every \(\nu>0\) on any time interval where its parameters and pressure are smooth.

#### B.5.1 Domain and Boundary Conditions

Whenever \(a(t)\ne0\), the full velocity grows linearly because of the imposed strain. For \(\Gamma_v\ne0\), even the swirl alone decays as \(1/r\) and is independent of \(z\). Every nonzero instantaneous member has infinite whole-space kinetic energy. On a finite cylinder, prescribe the displayed velocity traces. A stress formulation may instead take the stress traces consistent with the displayed pressure. The radial inflow and axial outflow balance by incompressibility. These are open-flow boundary data, not stationary no-slip walls.

The core enstrophy per unit axial length is finite:

\[
\mathcal Z_\ell=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R^2}|\boldsymbol\omega|^2\,dxdy
=\frac{\Gamma_v^2}{4\pi Q},
\qquad
\dot{\mathcal Z}_\ell=(2a-4\nu/Q)\mathcal Z_\ell.
\]

This two-dimensional cross-section accounting is not the closed three-dimensional global enstrophy balance. Axial transport through the ends matters. At the steady width, contraction, stretching, and diffusion are simultaneously active, while the core magnitude remains steady. Stabilization is therefore compatible with continuing motion.

#### B.5.2 Concentration and Imposed Strain

If \(a\) remains bounded on a finite interval, the explicit formula bounds \(Q\) away from zero and keeps the vortex amplitude finite. More generally, finite cumulative strain \(\int_0^T|a|dt<\infty\) gives a positive lower bound for \(Q\), although singular instantaneous \(a\) can still destroy regularity of the affine background.

Choose \(a=c/(T-t)\), \(c>0\). The width tends to zero as \(t\uparrow T\), with

where \(C>0\) depends on the initial width and interval. For nonzero circulation, this creates vorticity concentration within the known imposed-strain class. ### B.6 Exact Finite-Energy Periodic Cancellation

An independent admissible-domain example follows from a classical Beltrami construction. On \((\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^3\), set

\[
\mathbf v=(a_0\sin z+c_0\cos y,\ b_0\sin x+a_0\cos z,\ c_0\sin y+b_0\cos x).
\]

Direct differentiation verifies \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0\), \(\nabla\times\mathbf v=\mathbf v\), and \(\Delta\mathbf v=-\mathbf v\). The identity

\[
(\mathbf v\cdot\nabla)\mathbf v=\nabla(|\mathbf v|^2/2)-\mathbf v\times(\nabla\times\mathbf v)
\]

makes the nonlinear term a gradient. Hence

\[
\mathbf u=e^{-\nu t}\mathbf v,\qquad
p=-\tfrac12 e^{-2\nu t}|\mathbf v|^2,
\qquad \mathbf f=0
\]

is an exact smooth periodic solution, with initial condition \(\mathbf v\), finite energy per cell, and periodic pressure. Energy and enstrophy both decay by the factor \(e^{-2\nu t}\). The spatial integral of vortex stretching is zero, though pointwise stretching need not vanish. This is an independently checked example in an established external solution class; its equations are not claimed as native Zenetist formulas or new historical discoveries.

It shows that rotational organization can coexist with exact nonlinear cancellation and positive viscosity. It supplies a comparison field for studying one physical expression of orientation while χ retains its broader architectural scope.

### B.7 Conditional Enstrophy Estimates

On a periodic domain or for decaying smooth fields, put

\[
\mathcal Z=\tfrac12\|\boldsymbol\omega\|_2^2,
\quad \mathcal P=\int\boldsymbol\omega^T\mathsf S\boldsymbol\omega,
\quad \mathcal D=\|\nabla\boldsymbol\omega\|_2^2.
\]

The established balance is \(\mathcal Z'=\mathcal P-\nu\mathcal D\) in the unforced case. Suppose the physical articulation yields, for \(0<\delta\le1\),

\[
\mathcal P(t)\le(1-\delta)\nu\mathcal D(t)+g(t)\mathcal Z(t),
\qquad g\in L^1(0,T),\quad g\ge0.
\]

Then direct integration gives

\[
\mathcal Z(t)\le\mathcal Z(0)\exp\!\left(\int_0^t g(s)ds\right),
\qquad
\delta\nu\int_0^T\mathcal D(t)dt<\infty.
\]

This is a conditional budget estimate. Its hypothesis describes one **centropically constrained** mathematical regime: vortex-stretching production is prevented from outrunning the combination of positive viscous dissipation and an integrable growth allowance. If a Zenetist physical articulation yields such a relation, enstrophy and accumulated dissipation are controlled on the interval. It is a sufficient condition, not a universal Zenetist requirement that amplification must always remain bounded in this way.

Define \(s_+=\max(s,0)\) where vorticity is nonzero and set it to zero at vorticity zeros. A simpler sufficient hypothesis is \(\|s_+(\cdot,t)\|_\infty\in L^1(0,T)\), since \(\mathcal P\le2\|s_+\|_\infty\mathcal Z\). This records directional strain quantitatively as one physical component of the larger orientation problem.

#### B.7.1 Countervailing Motion and Entropic Self-Exhaustion

The architecture admits more than one relation between amplification and constraint. For every finite non-limit expression with \(0<\chi<\infty\), the `SP03-expression-ratio-mathematics.md` weights

\[

\]

are both nonzero. The pure \(\chi=0\) and \(\chi\to\infty\) cases remain limit expressions. Thus a non-limit lattice can contain countervailing centropic and entropic operations without either essence becoming the other and without the two orientations fusing.

This supplies one possible structural reading of rotational amplification: centropic motion can constrain, redirect, integrate, or stabilize an amplification process while entropic motion can intensify fragmentary tendencies. The physical sign of vortex stretching or viscosity does not determine that classification. The classification depends on what the motion does to the configuration, its relations, its available capacity, and its continuation. Viscosity therefore remains a positive physical dissipation term; its Zenetist standing is read from its function within the complete motion rather than identified with centropy by definition.

A second route is **entropic self-exhaustion**. Where amplification functions fragmentarily, the motion can erode the relative structural conditions that permit its own continuation. In that case, the entropic trajectory is not required to remain indefinitely controllable by a counter-motion. It can run toward collapse by consuming or destroying its own expressive support. **Absolute Dispersion names the terminal resolution after the relevant active motion exhausts; it is not identical to the instantaneous fact of large vorticity or large rotational amplitude.**

The fluid problem can therefore be approached through two mathematically distinct questions: whether a centropically constrained regime yields a bound such as the estimate in §B.7, and whether an entropically oriented regime admits a capacity, support-scale, regularity, or continuation variable whose erosion terminates the expressed configuration. The second possibility aligns naturally with the earlier distinction between growing local intensity and shrinking structural scale. Neither route requires a universal claim that every amplification is opposed in the same way.
