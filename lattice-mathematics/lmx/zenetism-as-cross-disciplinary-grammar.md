## 1. Cross-Disciplinary Purpose

Zenetism was developed as a cross-disciplinary grammar: an architecture of principles, relations, orientations, and operations through which different domains of expression can be examined together.

Its intended applications include biology, psychology, theology, physical processes, cognition, and symbolic interpretation. The disciplinary subject changes; the task is to identify how the relevant relations operate within that subject.

The foundational corpus already carries this scope. The Author's Preface in MP01 presents a deeper logic connecting myth, science, and mysticism. MP02's introduction describes an architecture intended to hold difference across domains of human understanding and to bring science, mysticism, philosophy, and symbol into relation.[1][2]

The grammar consists in the organization of the system: its primitives, their functions, the relations among them, the conditions of manifestation, and the different courses that expression can take.

A particular application therefore proceeds from the architecture into a specified domain. The application selects relevant relations and supplies the domain-specific articulation through which those relations can be examined.

> **The application is domain-specific. The grammar is cross-disciplinary.**

### A.3 A Localized Spiral-and-Axial Velocity Family

**Basis:** candidate geometry proposed during the discussion, using a stream-function representation. The particular Gaussian profile is an additional modeling choice, rather than an equation retrieved from the Zenetist corpus.

Let \(A,B,R,Z>0\), with \(A\) and \(B\) having units of inverse time and \(R,Z\) having units of length. Define

\[
G(r,z)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{r^2}{R^2}-\frac{z^2}{Z^2}\right),
\qquad
\Psi(r,z)=Ar^2zG(r,z).
\]

Set

\[
u_r=-\frac{1}{r}\partial_z\Psi,
\qquad
u_z=\frac{1}{r}\partial_r\Psi,
\qquad
u_\theta=BrG.
\]

The resulting velocity components are

\[
\boxed{
u_r=-ArG\left(1-\frac{2z^2}{Z^2}\right),
\quad
u_\theta=BrG,
\quad
u_z=2AzG\left(1-\frac{r^2}{R^2}\right).
}
\]

For \(|z|<Z/\sqrt{2}\), the radial component points toward the axis. For \(r<R\), the axial component has the sign of \(z\), producing opposed axial outflows. The azimuthal component supplies rotation. These statements describe the local geometry of the candidate; orientation assignments retain the arc-relative and functional definitions in §4.

**Incompressibility.** For \(r>0\),

\[
\nabla\cdot\mathbf u
=
\frac{1}{r}\partial_r(ru_r)+\partial_zu_z
=
-\frac{1}{r}\partial_r\partial_z\Psi
+\frac{1}{r}\partial_z\partial_r\Psi
=0.
\]

The identity extends through the axis by smoothness.

**Smoothness at the axis.** With \(r^2=x^2+y^2\), the same field is

\[
u_x=-AxG\left(1-\frac{2z^2}{Z^2}\right)-ByG,
\]

\[
u_y=-AyG\left(1-\frac{2z^2}{Z^2}\right)+BxG,
\]

\[
u_z=2AzG\left(1-\frac{x^2+y^2}{R^2}\right).
\]

Every component is a polynomial multiplied by a Gaussian. The field is smooth throughout \(\mathbb R^3\), and its spatial derivatives decay rapidly.

**Finite kinetic energy.** Direct integration yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal K
&=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R^3}|\mathbf u|^2\,d\mathbf x\\
&=\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_0^\infty
\left(u_r^2+u_\theta^2+u_z^2\right)r\,dr\,dz\\
&=\boxed{
\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\pi^{3/2}R^2Z}{64}
\left(3A^2R^2+4A^2Z^2+4B^2R^2\right).
}
\end{aligned}
\]

The three contributions inside the final parentheses arise respectively from radial, axial, and azimuthal motion. This formula was independently rechecked by integrating those contributions separately.

**Established result:** an explicit smooth, rapidly decaying, divergence-free, finite-energy family exhibiting central spiraling and opposed axial motion.

**Dynamical development:** determine the evolution of \(A,B,R,Z\), pressure, and momentum residual. Smooth time-dependent parameters with \(R,Z>0\) preserve the instantaneous spatial identities; their time evolution must satisfy the momentum equation. Energy behavior approaching any proposed critical time is evaluated from the displayed formula together with those evolution laws.

### A.4 Local Coupling of Contraction, Axial Extension, and Rotation

**Basis:** a direct calculation within the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations.[13] This local linear field and the localized Gaussian family are distinct constructions.

Consider

\[
u_r=-a(t)r,
\qquad
u_z=b(t)z,
\qquad
u_\theta=\Omega(t)r.
\]

Incompressibility gives

\[
\frac{1}{r}\partial_r(ru_r)+\partial_zu_z
=-2a+b=0,
\]

and consequently

\[
\boxed{b=2a.}
\]

For an axisymmetric field with no azimuthal forcing or azimuthal pressure gradient, the angular momentum equation is

\[
\partial_tu_\theta
+u_r\partial_ru_\theta
+u_z\partial_zu_\theta
+\frac{u_ru_\theta}{r}
=
\nu\left(\partial_r^2+\frac{1}{r}\partial_r+\partial_z^2-\frac{1}{r^2}\right)u_\theta.
\]

Substitution gives

\[
\dot\Omega r-2a\Omega r=0,
\qquad
\boxed{\dot\Omega=2a\Omega.}
\]

Thus

\[
\Omega(t)=\Omega(0)\exp\!\left(2\int_0^t a(s)\,ds\right).
\]

Radial contraction, axial extension, and rotational amplification are linked quantitatively in this field.

**Pressure completion.** The Cartesian velocity is

\[
\mathbf u=(-ax-\Omega y,\;\Omega x-ay,\;2az).
\]

A pressure that completes the unforced momentum equation is

\[
\boxed{
p(x,y,z,t)
=
\frac{\dot a-a^2+\Omega^2}{2}(x^2+y^2)
-(\dot a+2a^2)z^2+p_0(t).
}
\]

Direct substitution, with \(\dot\Omega=2a\Omega\), gives

\[
\partial_t\mathbf u+(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u+\nabla p=0,
\qquad
\Delta\mathbf u=0.
\]

For smooth \(a\) and \(\Omega\) on the interval considered, this is an exact local solution for every \(\nu>0\). Restricted to a bounded region, its boundary data are the traces of the displayed field. Its nonzero continuation across all of \(\mathbb R^3\) has infinite kinetic energy because the velocity grows linearly with distance. Its role here is local dynamical compatibility; §A.3 supplies the separate finite-energy candidate.

### A.5 Orientation-Dependent Vorticity Amplification

**Basis:** an established fluid-dynamics identity, written out for the present comparison. Vorticity alignment and the directional strain factor are treated explicitly in Galanti, Gibbon, and Heritage.[14]

For a smooth unforced incompressible flow, define

\[
\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf u,
\qquad
\mathsf S=\frac{\nabla\mathbf u+(\nabla\mathbf u)^T}{2}.
\]

Taking the curl of the momentum equation yields

\[
D_t\boldsymbol\omega
=\mathsf S\boldsymbol\omega+\nu\Delta\boldsymbol\omega.
\]

Where \(\boldsymbol\omega\ne0\), define the vortex-direction vector

\[
\mathbf n=\frac{\boldsymbol\omega}{|\boldsymbol\omega|}.
\]

Taking the scalar product with \(\boldsymbol\omega\) gives

\[
\boxed{
D_t\frac{|\boldsymbol\omega|^2}{2}
=
|\boldsymbol\omega|^2\mathbf n^T\mathsf S\mathbf n
+
\nu\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\Delta\boldsymbol\omega.
}
\]

The sign of \(\mathbf n^T\mathsf S\mathbf n\) specifies whether the local stretching contribution amplifies or reduces vorticity magnitude. Orientation relative to deformation therefore participates in the physical evolution itself.

For periodic fields, or sufficiently smooth decaying fields for which the boundary contributions vanish, integration by parts gives the enstrophy balance

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\int|\boldsymbol\omega|^2\,d\mathbf x
=
\int\boldsymbol\omega^T\mathsf S\boldsymbol\omega\,d\mathbf x
-
\nu\int|\nabla\boldsymbol\omega|^2\,d\mathbf x.
}
\]

For a forced flow, the right-hand side acquires \(\int\boldsymbol\omega\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf f)\,d\mathbf x\).

The variable \(\mathbf n\) has the local physical meaning defined here. Its proposed relation to the broader Zenetist orientation parameter belongs to the explicit physical realization. The formula provides a quantitative site for examining coupled orientation, stretching, and viscous dissipation.
