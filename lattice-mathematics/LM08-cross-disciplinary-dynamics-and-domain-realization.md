## 8. Reserve-Compatible Simultaneous Transfer

### 8.1 From One Boundary to a Composite Exchange

### 8.2 Construction

For each constituent i, choose a reserve floor rᵢ, a capacity cᵢ, and a current amount qᵢ satisfying

\[

\]

Let pᵢⱼ be the proposed amount transferred from i to j, with

\[

\]

Forbidden or unavailable edges have pᵢⱼ = 0. Define available surplus sᵢ, reception room kᵢ, total proposed outflow Pᵢ, and total proposed inflow Qᵢ:

\[
s_i=q_i-r_i,\qquad k_i=c_i-q_i,
\qquad P_i=\sum_jp_{ij},\qquad Q_i=\sum_jp_{ji}.
\]

Define the donor and recipient factors

\[
\alpha_i=
\begin{cases}
\min(1,s_i/P_i),&P_i>0,\\
1,&P_i=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\beta_i=
\begin{cases}
\min(1,k_i/Q_i),&Q_i>0,\\
1,&Q_i=0.
\end{cases}
\]

The executed transfer is

\[
\boxed{T_{ij}=p_{ij}\min(\alpha_i,\beta_j).}
\]

The simultaneous update is

\[
\boxed{q_i^+=q_i-\sum_jT_{ij}+\sum_jT_{ji}.}
\]

The factors depend on the beginning-of-step state and the entire proposed exchange. They implement a joint reserve and reception condition while keeping each donor, recipient, and edge identifiable.

### 8.3 Admissibility and Conservation

**Theorem 8.1 — Reserve-compatible simultaneous transfer.** The construction has the following properties:

\[
T_{ij}\ge0,\qquad
\sum_jT_{ij}\le q_i-r_i,\qquad
\sum_jT_{ji}\le c_i-q_i,
\]

\[
\boxed{r_i\le q_i^+\le c_i,\qquad \sum_iq_i^+=\sum_iq_i.}
\]

It preserves every zero proposed edge.

**Proof.** Nonnegativity follows from the definitions. For each donor,

\[
\sum_jT_{ij}\le\alpha_i\sum_jp_{ij}\le s_i.
\]

The same argument at each recipient gives \(\sum_jT_{ji}\le\beta_iQ_i\le k_i\). Therefore

\[
q_i^+\ge q_i-s_i=r_i,
\qquad
q_i^+\le q_i+k_i=c_i.
\]

Summing the update cancels every executed internal transfer once as donor loss and once as recipient gain. A zero pᵢⱼ gives a zero Tᵢⱼ. ∎

The theorem is exact for the stated allocation. It supplies a sufficient joint-admissibility construction: beginning-of-step surplus and room are respected before any incoming transfer is counted as newly available. A within-step recirculation admitting more than this requires its own temporal ordering and budget account.

### 8.4 Explicit Three-Constituent Example

\qquad p_{12}=p_{13}=1,
\]

with all other proposals zero. The donor factor is α₁ = 1/2, and both relevant recipient factors are one. Thus

\qquad q^+=\left(0,\frac12,\frac12\right).

The total remains one, each recipient receives an admissible amount, and the donor retains its specified reserve floor. Giving the donor a strictly positive floor changes the executed total accordingly.
