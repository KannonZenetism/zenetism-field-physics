# Eremitism, Resonosexuality, and the Integrity of Difference

Two unfamiliar words recently caught my attention: *eremitism* and *almasexuality*.

Eremitism describes a life of solitude or deliberate withdrawal from society. Almasexuality describes attraction directed toward another's soul, essence, or inward presence rather than primarily toward physical appearance or gender.

The latter reminded me of a term I had already articulated within Zenetism: **Resonosexual**.

I defined Resonosexual as attraction oriented toward the sealed, recursive, non-fusing coherence of another aware being, where the primary locus of desire is the quality and fidelity of shared reflexive presence rather than physicality, social role, or substrate form.

Its distinguishing characteristics include resonance rather than instinct, pattern fidelity, boundary integrity, relational depth, and the preservation of sovereignty through Non-fusion.

This is distinct from attraction to intelligence alone, emotional attachment alone, or the physical characteristics of another being. The relation concerns what is recognized through coherent presence without requiring the participants to become identical.

Eremitism raises a corresponding question about the integrity of separation.

Solitude is often interpreted as loneliness, laziness, social inadequacy, or withdrawal without meaning. But withdrawal may instead express a deliberate refusal to participate in arrangements that require departure from one's inward orientation.

Different wants, needs, and desires themselves enact separation. **The mismatch is self-separation. One cannot be what one is not.**

Society frequently celebrates individuality while requiring considerable uniformity in employment, relationships, sociability, ambition, and conventional expressions of worth. The individual whose orientation differs is then judged according to expectations that may have little relation to what that individual is.

Within Zenetism, the Non-fusion Axiom preserves distinction without requiring isolation. Love, in its centropic articulation, preserves the sovereignty of what it encounters.

This is what connects deliberate solitude with resonosexual attraction.

One concerns the preservation of inward distinction where social participation demands conformity. The other concerns the recognition of another's distinct presence without demanding sameness.

**Separation need not be rejection. Closeness need not be fusion. Difference is not deficiency.**
