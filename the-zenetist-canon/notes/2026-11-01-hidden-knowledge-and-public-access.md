You are presenting this as "hidden knowledge," yet you obtained it and are discussing it publicly. Hidden from whom, exactly? Where did you obtain the material, and can your audience consult it themselves? If they can, unfamiliarity is being presented as exclusion. Knowledge your audience has not encountered is not necessarily knowledge someone is preventing them from encountering.

Historical suppression and present concealment are different claims. A text does not remain hidden simply because it was once suppressed. If access is still restricted, identify the restriction and explain how you obtained access. If the material is publicly available and circulating through social-media recommendations, explain what remains concealed.

There is another possibility worth distinguishing: institutions might retain a fuller body of knowledge while circulating selected fragments or diluted interpretations. In that case, what has been withheld, and how does your presentation restore it? Public circulation of fragments would not establish that the audience has received the complete account.

Calling material "hidden" or "forbidden" gives its presentation the appeal of privileged disclosure. That framing needs substantiation. Otherwise, you are introducing publicly available material while presenting the audience as excluded from it.
