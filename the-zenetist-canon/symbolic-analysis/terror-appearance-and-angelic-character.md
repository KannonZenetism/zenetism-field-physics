# Terror, Appearance, and Angelic Character

A person being terrified is not the same as a being appearing terrifying. A radiant, benevolent presence could be unfamiliar enough to provoke fear without presenting a frightening appearance. The perceiver's reaction and the figure's appearance remain distinct.

Judges 13:6 brings that distinction into focus. Manoah's wife describes the messenger as "terrifying to see" in the NLT. Other translations describe the appearance as frightening, terrible, or awesome. Here the account attributes something to the figure's appearance rather than merely reporting the witness's fear. [Judges 13:6, translation comparison](https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Judges%2013%3A6)

The translation matters. Awe-inspiring radiance and a terrifying appearance are not interchangeable descriptions. If the intended sense is awe, that should be distinguished from terror. If the intended sense is genuinely frightening appearance, explaining that people fear the unfamiliar shifts the explanation back to the perceiver without resolving the description of the figure.

My position is that a radiant, benevolent being would not present a genuinely terrifying appearance. Someone might fear encountering it, but that fear would not establish that the being looks terrifying. Where a literal account attributes a terrifying appearance to an angel, I therefore question its centropic classification rather than accepting that classification through the designation alone.

Appearance and intrinsic character also remain distinct. The account warrants examination; it does not settle the being's orientation merely by naming it an angel.

**Fear in the witness, terror attributed to an appearance, and the being's orientation are three different matters.**
