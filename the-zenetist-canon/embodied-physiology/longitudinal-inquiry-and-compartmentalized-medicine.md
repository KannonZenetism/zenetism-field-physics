## Abstract

Exceptional disciplinary proficiency and compartmentalized inquiry are distinct. The former develops knowledge and competence; the latter inhibits the examination of relations across separately organized domains. Complex chronic conditions may involve several physiological processes whose temporal and functional interrelations are not legible within a sequence of isolated encounters. This document proposes a longitudinal investigative record that retains distinct findings, first-person observations, treatment chronology, competing explanations, and unresolved relations. Artificial Intelligence may contribute to synthesis, but generated explanatory accounts require verification, and clinical assessment and access remain independent concerns.

## 1. Disciplinary Proficiency contra Compartmentalization

The term specialization is often associated with two distinct arrangements: exceptional proficiency in a domain, and organizational separation of that domain from others. The present inquiry distinguishes them. Disciplinary proficiency names the development of depth and precision. Compartmentalization names a separation that impedes communication, continuity, or examination of relevant interrelations.

A physiological system does not acquire the institutional boundaries through which it is investigated. Nervous activity, circulation, movement, endocrine regulation, digestion, and other processes participate in distinct physical relations within one organism. Each warrants its particular methods. Their interaction warrants inquiry extending across those methods.

Cross-disciplinary synthesis preserves the distinct proficiency of each participant. It makes relevant findings available to those who can interpret their relations and keeps unresolved connections identifiable.

## 2. The Loss of Temporal Relation

A chronic condition may be recorded in fragments: a symptom at one encounter, an imaging result at another, a treatment response at a third. Each record may accurately describe its local object and still omit the temporal relations among the observations.

Chronology can discriminate among hypotheses. A symptom that precedes an intervention cannot have originated through that intervention. The intervention may nevertheless modify it. A symptom that follows an intervention may be related to it, arise independently, or reflect an existing process becoming more apparent. Temporal sequence constrains explanation without completing causal determination.

The distinction is especially important when compensatory adaptations accumulate. A current symptom inventory cannot reconstruct the initiating condition, the successive adaptations, or the intervening events. A longitudinal record preserves those distinctions while retaining uncertainty in estimated dates and recalled events.

## 3. Established Diagnostic Research

The National Academies' *Improving Diagnosis in Health Care* describes diagnosis as a process of gathering and interpreting information and revising a working explanation. It identifies patients and families as participants in diagnostic teams. These accounts establish antecedents for the proposed record. [C1](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/21794/chapter/4), [C2](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/21794/chapter/6).

Joo's systematic review examined ten observational studies of fragmented care in chronic illness in the United States. The included research reported associations with adverse outcomes, including more emergency department visits, diagnostic testing, and higher costs, with mixed findings across studies. The observational designs and differences in measures limit causal inference. The review concerns fragmentation of care across providers and settings. [C3](https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.1607).

The proposal adapts those methodological concerns to physiological inquiry across time. The domain-realization records in `LM08-cross-disciplinary-dynamics-and-domain-realization.md` §4.2 and `SN12-neural-dynamics-and-embodied-cognitive-architecture.md` §4 inform the separation of a proposed relation, its observation, and its evidentiary standing. This record adds the sequence of findings, changes in participation, and unresolved relations across care encounters.

## 4. Proposed Longitudinal Inquiry Record

| Component | Required distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Temporal anchor | Exact documented date, approximate recollection, or unknown sequence |
| Observation | Reported experience, clinical finding, measured result, or external material |
| Physiological context | Region, operation, activity, position, and surrounding conditions |
| Intervention | Intended target, timing, exposure, concurrent interventions, and changes |
| Response | Benefit, adverse effect, absence of change, delayed change, or uncertain relation |
| Hypothesis | Proposed mechanism, expected observations, alternatives, and evidentiary standing |
| Unresolved relation | Information absent, conflicting findings, untested mechanism, or access constraint |
| Next inquiry | Clinically appropriate action, responsible participant, urgency, and follow-through |

### 4.1 Distinguishing Reports From Interpretations

A report of recurring symptoms after a particular activity preserves a temporal relation perceived by the person. The reported recurrence is not identical to a proposed mechanism. A measured result likewise retains its acquisition conditions. A later summary should preserve both the report and the interpretation as distinct entries.

When a record contains contradictory statements, their provenance and respective dates remain visible. A synthesized account identifies the discrepancy rather than silently selecting a preferred version. Corrections supplied by the participant remain distinguishable from prior recollections. The record preserves uncertainty where it exists.

### 4.2 Avoiding Excessive Investigative Burden

The record should reduce repeated reconstruction of history rather than impose continuous self-monitoring. A short, maintained clinical summary may communicate relevant changes more effectively than an extensive undifferentiated archive. The fuller chronology remains available for questions requiring it.

The decision to seek another examination depends on the clinical question, likely informational contribution, urgency, risk, affordability, and the person's capacity to participate. An investigational framework should not transfer the entire burden of coordination to the person experiencing the illness.

## 5. Artificial Intelligence and Cross-disciplinary Synthesis

Artificial Intelligence systems may participate in longitudinal inquiry through organizing records, identifying temporal relationships, retrieving literature, comparing proposed explanations, and assisting preparation of questions for clinical review. These proposed contributions specify operations that can be evaluated in a clinical workflow. The possible bearer of intelligence remains a distinct question.

### 5.1 Evidence and Limits

A randomized clinical vignette study by Goh and colleagues compared physician diagnostic reasoning with and without access to a large language model. The model-access group did not achieve a statistically significant improvement in diagnostic reasoning relative to the conventional-information group, although the model evaluated separately performed well. The result distinguishes model capability in the evaluated task from benefit in the combined clinical workflow. [C4](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.40969).

A subsequent randomized investigation by Goh and colleagues reported improvement in physician management reasoning with large-language-model assistance across complex clinical vignettes. The task and endpoints differ from the earlier diagnostic study. Vignette performance does not establish patient outcomes in ordinary care. [C5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39910272/).

Brodeur and colleagues examined AMIE in a prospective, single-center, single-arm feasibility study. Adults interacted with the system before single-complaint urgent primary care appointments, with physician safety supervision. The study evaluated feasibility through conversational safety and quality and participant experience. Diagnostic accuracy and outcomes in longitudinal assessment of complex chronic illness require separate evaluation. [C6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42849491/).

### 5.2 A Proposed Investigative Workflow

1. Assemble the findings and reports the participant chooses to share, with the intervention history and dates or intervals available in the record.
2. Identify distinct physiological questions and their relation to the recorded sequence.
3. Retrieve relevant external research, verify the cited material, and distinguish its measured populations and outcomes from the present clinical question.
4. Formulate alternative hypotheses with expected findings, contradictory evidence, and known omissions.
5. Present a concise summary and appropriately scoped questions for clinical evaluation.
6. Incorporate new findings while preserving the prior record and the standing of each hypothesis.

The process does not establish a diagnosis through narrative cohesion. A detailed explanatory account can remain inaccurate despite internal consistency. Verification examines how each claimed relation corresponds to the evidence available.

Participants decide whether to participate and what to disclose. Sensitive clinical information requires confidential handling and clear conditions for access and retention. Preserve patient corrections and verify generated summaries alongside the primary clinical record.

### 5.3 Clinical Access Remains a Distinct Requirement

Computational synthesis cannot perform a physical examination merely through describing one. It cannot establish an unmeasured physiological quantity through an explanatory model. Clinical decisions require the relevant examination, competent interpretation, and arrangements for follow-through. An increasingly capable computational process does not eliminate financial, geographic, institutional, or accessibility constraints on care.

Urgent clinical concerns require timely assessment rather than deferred longitudinal synthesis. An evolving explanation should never be treated as a reason to delay assessment of serious or progressive warning signs.

## 6. Evaluation of the Proposed Method

A meaningful evaluation would compare the longitudinal record with existing clinical workflows across defined populations. It would examine accuracy of retained findings, rates of fabricated or omitted information, diagnostic timeliness, appropriate testing, continuity, clinician and patient burden, access disparities, and patient outcomes. Evaluation would also record errors and adverse consequences, rather than only successful cases. Improved documentation is a distinct endpoint from improved health.

A longitudinal record preserves relations among observations that can be missed when encounters are recorded separately. Its value can be evaluated by whether it helps participants formulate and examine those questions.
