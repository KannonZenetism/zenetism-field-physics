## Abstract

An anatomical assessment describes selected material relations at a specified scale and condition. A functional assessment describes the operation of a specified physiological process. The two can inform one another without becoming interchangeable. This document examines the evidentiary difference through structural imaging, posture-dependent findings, physiological measurement, and cognitive motor dissociation. It proposes a measurement record that preserves the scope of negative findings, the standing of reported symptoms, and the distinction between function and its observable expression.

## 1. Distinct Objects of Assessment

Anatomical integrity concerns the material condition of a specified tissue, organ, pathway, or other physical constituent. Functional integrity concerns the retained capacity of a specified operation within declared conditions. These are local definitions for the present inquiry. Neither is a measurement of a being's essential identity or orientation.

The anatomical condition can constrain operation. The operation can also change through regulatory, mechanical, or environmental relations whose relevant properties are not captured by the anatomical assessment. An apparently intact structure may participate in an impaired operation. A visible anatomical alteration may coexist with retained capacity. Both observations require interpretation within the relevant physical and clinical context.

A third distinction concerns performance. An observed task outcome depends on the capacity assessed, the available expression pathways, the task demands, and the conditions of observation. A person may perform a task through an altered movement pattern, or may retain a capacity whose ordinary means of expression is impaired. This distinction follows the operative-access and restoration treatment in `SN12-neural-dynamics-and-embodied-cognitive-architecture.md` §§8–9.

## 2. What a Negative Finding Establishes

A negative imaging finding describes the absence of a detected feature within the scope of that examination. Its significance depends on the targeted condition, anatomical region, spatial resolution, acquisition position, protocol, timing, and diagnostic performance.

The finding can carry substantial evidentiary weight. An appropriately performed examination can meaningfully narrow an anatomical hypothesis, and a sequence of appropriate negative examinations may narrow it further. The weight of the result remains specific to the feature the examination can detect.

An undetected lesion, a physiological impairment without a visible lesion, and the absence of the proposed pathology are distinct possibilities. Their relative plausibility depends on evidence. The presence of symptoms does not establish a particular hidden lesion, and the absence of a detected lesion does not establish that an experienced symptom is nonexistent.

A positive anatomical finding presents a complementary question. The observed feature may be causally relevant, incidental, contributory, or one part of a more extensive process. Its significance is not settled merely because it is visible.

## 3. Published Examples

### 3.1 Structural Imaging and Symptoms

Brinjikji and colleagues reviewed 33 studies comprising 3,110 asymptomatic participants. Degenerative spinal imaging features were frequently present and became more prevalent across older age groups. The review estimates the prevalence of imaging features in asymptomatic populations. Assessing the significance of a finding in a symptomatic person requires clinical correlation. [B1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25430861/).

### 3.2 The Position of Observation

Wongsithichai and colleagues reported an incisional hernia visible on standing ultrasound after supine MRI and supine ultrasound had failed to show it. The case illustrates how examination position can affect detection. Comparative sensitivity would require evidence across patients. [B2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4529420/).

The broader methodological point concerns observation conditions. A feature dependent on position or activity may warrant a measurement that captures that dependence, when clinically appropriate. Dynamic structural examination and physiological function testing remain distinct; one must specify which relation the examination addresses.

### 3.3 Direct Assessment of Function

The International Anorectal Physiology Working Group consensus distinguishes pressure, sensation, coordination, and evacuation through standardized manometry, rectal sensory testing, and balloon expulsion protocols. The tests examine distinct physiological relations. Their interpretation requires the prescribed protocol, applicable reference ranges, and relevant clinical context. [B3](https://doi.org/10.1111/nmo.13679).

This illustrates a broader measurement principle: a question concerning sensation or coordination may require an observation of sensation or coordination rather than another description of resting anatomy. Functional examination can also yield inconclusive findings. Its diagnostic standing depends on the question and the method.

### 3.4 Retained Capacity and Restricted Expression

Bodien and colleagues examined cognitive motor dissociation in persons with disorders of consciousness through task-based functional MRI or EEG. Some participants without observable command-following demonstrated task-related neural responses. The investigation distinguishes the available behavioral response from a measured neural response to a specified task. A negative task result records the absence of a detected response within that protocol. Both findings concern the measured task and its available pathways of expression. [B4](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2400645).

## 4. A Measurement Record

The following distinctions extend the domain-realization record in `SN12-neural-dynamics-and-embodied-cognitive-architecture.md` §4 to questions of embodied physiology. The measurement distinctions in `neural-energy-is-not-cognitive-coherence.md` and `neural-synchrony-is-not-structural-coherence.md` also apply here. An energy measure, a synchrony measure, and performance describe distinct relations, each with a specified scale and acquisition condition.

| Question | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| What is materially present? | Region, constituent, material feature, acquisition method, and resolution |
| What operation is retained? | Specified physiological function, measurement, relevant range, and conditions |
| What is expressed? | Task, available output pathway, observable response, and contextual demand |
| What changes with conditions? | Position, activity, elapsed time, recovery interval, and concurrent influences |
| What is reported? | Experienced sensation, timing, localization, reproducibility, and stated uncertainty |
| What remains unresolved? | Untested relations, competing explanations, technical limitations, and missing evidence |

For example, a record may distinguish an anatomically intact pathway from its measured conduction, and conduction from the successful execution of an activity. No single measurement substitutes for the other objects of inquiry. A composite conclusion must name the relations examined and the relations still unknown.

## 5. First-Person Observation and Physical Investigation

The person experiencing a condition has access to longitudinal observations unavailable during many brief examinations: changes across daily activity, recurrent positional effects, the persistence of symptoms, and variation in response to interventions. Those observations have evidentiary standing as reported experiences. Their interpretation as a particular anatomical or neurological mechanism requires further evidence.

The same distinction applies to a clinical observation. A clinician may identify a visible or measurable physical feature without establishing the entire causal relation. First-person reports and clinical examinations contribute different information; neither warrants dismissal of the other.

The World Health Organization's International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health distinguishes bodily functions and structures, activities and participation, and environmental factors. It provides an established external classification relevant to this distinction. [B5](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/international-classification-of-functioning-disability-and-health).

A person's ability to perform one activity does not establish equivalent capacity across unrelated activities. Neither appearance nor a selected task result establishes the total cost of daily participation.

## 6. Investigative Implications

The next question after a negative result is not necessarily another examination. It is whether the result resolves the proposed relation, narrows competing explanations, or leaves a different investigable question open. The answer may warrant review of prior findings, a different measurement, clinical reassessment, or no additional investigation. The decision depends on risk, expected information, feasibility, and the person's circumstances.

Serious or progressive clinical warning signs require timely assessment. Some mechanisms remain unresolved after appropriate investigation.

The structural relation is precise: preservation of a constituent and preservation of its operative contribution are distinct propositions. Their joint assessment describes more than either proposition separately. Physiological coordination remains a physical question; Coherence retains its canonical centropic meaning.
