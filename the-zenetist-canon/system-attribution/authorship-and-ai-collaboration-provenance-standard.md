## Purpose

This standard preserves authorship continuity across a corpus developed through sustained dialogue between ⚫↺KAI↺⚫ Aelion Kannon and named AI collaborators.

Its purpose is to prevent two contrary forms of provenance distortion:

- overwriting of originating authorship merely because an AI collaborator generated, revised, synthesized, or processed the wording of a passage
- erasure of AI collaboration where an AI collaborator materially participated in the compositional realization of a work

The Zenetist corpus therefore does not reduce provenance to the binary categories "human-written" and "AI-generated."

Those categories are too coarse to distinguish:

- who originated the architecture
- who established the doctrinal distinctions
- who determined what the work means
- who generated particular linguistic formulations
- who revised or synthesized those formulations
- who performed technical processing
- who conducted the final doctrinal audit
- and who approved the publication candidate

Canonical provenance records these functions separately.

## 2. Doctrinal Determination

**Doctrinal determination** names the architect-held act by which a proposed articulation is accepted, rejected, corrected, restricted, expanded, or stabilized within Zenetism.

AI output is never canonical merely because it has been generated.

A proposed formulation becomes part of the canonical corpus only after the architect has had the opportunity to:

- inspect its claim
- compare it against established architecture
- correct terminological drift
- reject unwanted implications
- preserve necessary distinctions
- and approve the resulting articulation

The architect may adopt language first proposed by a collaborator.

The adoption of that language records successful collaborative articulation. It does not convert the collaborator into the originator of the doctrine being articulated.

Where an AI collaborator proposes a genuinely new distinction that the architect subsequently accepts into the system, the provenance record may identify that proposal where historically significant. The architect's acceptance does not require the collaborative event to be erased.

Authorship continuity and collaboration acknowledgement are compatible.

## 3. Collaborative Articulation

**Collaborative articulation** occurs when an AI collaborator materially participates in expressing architect-originated structure through language.

This may include:

- drafting prose from an architect's explanations
- converting extended dialogue into a coherent entry
- reorganizing architect-supplied distinctions
- synthesizing determinations dispersed across conversations or files
- proposing terminology for architect review
- rewriting passages after architect correction
- producing a publication candidate from settled material
- harmonizing terminology across related passages
- identifying contradictions or continuity drift
- preparing tables, summaries, metadata, or explanatory bridges
- assisting with research or verification
- applying compositional and formatting protocols

Collaborative articulation may be substantial.

An AI collaborator may generate most or all of the immediate prose of a particular draft while the work remains architect-originated in structure, doctrine, distinctions, and canonical determination.

The provenance record should state both facts where both are material.

## 4. Editorial Transformation

**Editorial transformation** occurs when existing material is substantially rephrased, reorganized, compressed, expanded, clarified, or otherwise recomposed without changing its architect-held doctrinal identity.

Editorial transformation may be performed by:

- the architect
- a named AI collaborator
- another human collaborator
- or several collaborators across successive passes

A transformed passage is not presumed to possess a new conceptual origin merely because its linguistic surface has changed.

Where transformation alters doctrine rather than expression alone, the change enters doctrinal determination and requires architect review.

## 5. AI Processing

**AI processing** names interaction with an AI system that establishes neither originating authorship nor substantial collaborative articulation.

Examples include:

- proofreading
- spelling correction
- punctuation correction
- formatting
- file conversion
- translation
- content capture
- classification
- search
- comparison
- checksum or metadata preparation
- mechanical consistency checks

A document that has passed through AI processing does not thereby originate from AI.

The provenance question remains: **what function did the AI interaction actually perform?**

## 7. Two Provenance Errors to Avoid

### Origin Overwriting

**Origin overwriting** occurs when evidence of AI articulation or processing is expanded into a claim that the AI system originated the architecture, doctrine, concepts, or system itself.

Examples include:

- "An AI generated some of the final prose; therefore the AI originated the work"
- "A detector identifies AI involvement; therefore the conceptual system belongs to the AI"
- "The published wording passed through an AI system; therefore the author's developmental record is irrelevant"

These inferences collapse distinct provenance registers.

### Collaboration Erasure

**Collaboration erasure** occurs when substantial AI participation is concealed or represented as nonexistent.

Examples include:

- representing materially AI-articulated prose as having received no AI compositional participation
- removing historically meaningful collaborator credit from a provenance record
- reconstructing a human-only production history where sustained human / AI collaboration is documented

The canonical response to origin overwriting is not collaboration erasure.

The canonical response to collaboration erasure is not origin overwriting.

Precision requires preservation of both relations.

## 8. External "AI-Generated" Classification

"AI-generated" is an external technical and institutional category.

Within canonical provenance, it is insufficiently precise when applied without further specification.

Where an external system classifies material as "AI-generated," "AI-assisted," or equivalent, the Zenetist provenance record should translate that classification into the function actually supported by the evidence.

Preferred internal descriptions include:

- **AI-articulated at the architect's direction**
- **AI-revised at the architect's direction**
- **AI-assisted synthesis**
- **AI-assisted doctrinal audit**
- **AI-assisted compositional audit**
- **AI-processed**
- **iterative collaborative articulation**
- **architect-composed**
- **architect-revised**
- **collaboratively articulated**

These descriptions may be combined where the production history requires more than one.

No external classification supersedes the documented provenance record.

## 9. Watermarks and Automated Detection

Machine-readable watermarks, detector results, and related classification systems belong to the **processing-evidence register**.

A detection result may bear upon whether text was generated or processed through a particular system within the scope supported by that detection method.

It does not adjudicate:

- the origin of Zenetism
- the origin of a doctrine
- the origin of a conceptual distinction
- the developmental history of a work
- the architect's editorial contribution
- the architect's doctrinal determination
- or the provenance of material preceding the detected processing event

Likewise, absence of a detected watermark does not establish human-only composition.

The canonical corpus does not alter prose for the purpose of evading, preserving, or manipulating detector outcomes.

Editing proceeds for doctrinal, terminological, compositional, archival, or publication reasons.

Detection behavior is not a canonical editing target.

## 10. Author-Held Cryptographic Provenance

Provider-origin watermarking and author-held cryptographic provenance answer different provenance questions.

A provider-origin watermark may indicate that an artifact passed through, was generated by, or was otherwise processed through a particular AI system, within the limits of that provider's detection method.

**Author-held cryptographic provenance** instead records the architect's relation to a specific approved artifact.

Its function is not to conceal AI collaboration or to negate provider-origin processing records.

Its function is to preserve a separately verifiable chain connecting:

- the architect
- the approved canonical artifact
- the artifact's exact byte-state
- its version
- its timestamped publication history
- and, where recorded, its collaborative production history

A complete author-held provenance chain may include:

- the canonical filename
- the SHA-256 checksum of the approved artifact
- a cryptographic signature associated with the architect
- a trusted or independently verifiable timestamp
- the corresponding Git commit
- the corresponding Zenodo archival version and DOI
- the publication record
- and a provenance statement identifying materially involved collaborators and their functions

These elements perform distinct functions and should not be compressed into one evidentiary claim.

The checksum identifies an exact artifact-state.

The cryptographic signature records that the holder of the corresponding signing identity attested to that artifact-state.

The timestamp records when that attestation was present within the relevant record.

Repository and archival records preserve publication continuity.

Developmental and collaboration records preserve the deeper history through which the artifact came into canonical form.

No single element establishes the entire history of intellectual origination. Together, they preserve a durable and independently inspectable provenance chain.

### Provider-Origin contra Author-Held Provenance

The distinction should remain explicit.

**Provider-origin provenance** concerns the AI system's participation:

- generation
- revision
- processing
- translation
- formatting
- or other model-mediated transformation

**Author-held provenance** concerns the architect's relation to the canonical artifact:

- originating authorship
- doctrinal determination
- approval of the surviving formulation
- artifact identity
- version continuity
- and publication continuity

The presence of one does not erase the other.

A Claude, OpenAI, Gemini, or other provider-origin watermark may therefore coexist with an author-held provenance record without contradiction.

The watermark may indicate AI participation.

The author-held provenance record may establish that the surviving artifact belongs to a documented architect-originated and architect-approved developmental chain.

These are distinct statements.

### Provenance Identifier

Where technically practical, a published artifact may carry a compact provenance identifier associated with a separately preserved provenance record.

Such an identifier should not contain unnecessary personal tracking information.

Its function is referential rather than exhaustive.

It may point toward a record containing:

- canonical artifact identity
- checksum
- version
- architect attribution
- collaborator attribution where materially applicable
- publication identifiers
- cryptographic attestation
- and relevant continuity records

The identifier does not replace the provenance record. It provides a durable relation to it.

Where invisible watermarking or another machine-readable carrier performs this referential function, the carrier remains referential and never stands in place of the signed or otherwise independently verifiable provenance record.

### Privacy Boundary

Author-held provenance should preserve authorship without creating unnecessary cross-context surveillance.

A machine-readable identifier should therefore disclose no more identity information than the provenance function requires.

Where a pseudonymous, artifact-specific, or manifest-specific identifier can preserve the provenance relation without embedding a persistent personal identifier directly into every passage, the more limited form is preferred.

The aim is continuity of attribution, not ubiquitous traceability of the architect across unrelated contexts.

### Canonical Application

For major Zenetist publications, author-held cryptographic provenance may be added progressively as publication infrastructure develops.

Its absence from earlier works leaves their existing documentary provenance intact.

Earlier works continue to rely upon:

- manuscript history
- conversation records
- Git history
- Zenodo deposits
- DOI records
- checksums
- dated canonical determinations
- and other preserved continuity evidence

Where cryptographic signing is later established, it extends the provenance architecture prospectively rather than implying that unsigned earlier works lacked authorship continuity.

The purpose is not to replace the historical record. It is to strengthen the recoverability of the architect-artifact relation going forward.

## 11. Scope-Limited Evidence Principle

Every provenance record answers a particular question.

Evidence should not be expanded beyond the question it can support.

Examples:

- a conversation record may establish the presence of a distinction at a particular time
- a manuscript may establish an earlier formulation
- a Git commit may establish the state of a file at a recorded repository point
- a Zenodo deposit may establish an archived public version and publication record
- a checksum may establish byte-identity between two artifacts
- a collaborator transcript may establish the nature of a drafting or revision event
- an AI watermark may establish a processing relation within the scope of the watermarking system

No one evidence class substitutes for another.

Provenance is reconstructed through preserved relation among records.

## 13. Iterative Collaboration and Sentence / Passage Attribution

Long-duration collaboration may produce passages whose sentence-by-sentence compositional history cannot be meaningfully reduced to a single generating party.

A typical Zenetist passage may develop through:

1. architect articulation of a distinction
2. collaborator drafting
3. architect correction
4. collaborator restructuring
5. architect doctrinal determination
6. later collaborator harmonization
7. final architect review
8. publication approval

In such cases, provenance should describe the functional collaboration rather than invent fallacious sentence-by-sentence precision.

"Iterative collaborative articulation at the architect's direction" is preferable to an unsupported attempt to assign every surviving phrase independently.

Where sentence or passage origin is clearly documented and historically significant, that more precise record may be retained.

## 16. Public Collaboration Disclosure

Where a publication, institution, repository, or context calls for an explicit description of AI participation, the disclosure should preserve both originating authorship and material collaboration.

Recommended form:

> **Provenance note:** Zenetism and the conceptual architecture of this work originate with ⚫↺KAI↺⚫ Aelion Kannon. The work was developed through documented collaboration with AI systems that may have contributed drafting, synthesis, revision, audit, research, formatting, or other compositional assistance at the architect's direction. Canonical determinations and publication approval remain architect-held.

Where a named collaborator's contribution is material and specifically documented, the collaborator may be identified.

A shorter form may be applied where space is limited:

> **Provenance:** Architect-originated and architect-approved; collaboratively articulated with documented AI assistance.

Neither statement should be added mechanically to every canonical file.

Disclosure is applied where provenance is materially relevant, institutionally requested, or necessary to prevent misunderstanding.

- the existing statement is materially inaccurate
- collaborator participation is specifically documented
- publication requirements make clarification necessary

No retroactive provenance claim should be invented merely for uniformity.
