## Definition

**Radical Dogmatism** names the stance that treats an interpretive framework or epistemic primitive as establishing absolute certainty, while excluding its claimed finality from examination or correction.

The term identifies the absolutization of epistemic standing. It distinguishes a claim to finality from confidence, relative warrant, and explanatory power.

Its scope includes sensory, scientific, religious, philosophical, and metaphysical claims. An explicit doctrine is not required: perception or the apparent immediacy of experience may receive the same exemption from examination.

## Frameworks and Epistemic Primitives

Radical Dogmatism has two distinguishable applications:

- **Framework finality:** a discipline, doctrine, or interpretive system is treated as infallible, with its conditionality excluded from examination
- **Primitive indubitability:** the apparent availability of a subject, object, perception, experience, inference, or relation is treated as establishing its absolute metaphysical standing

Radical Skepticism examines the conditionality of interpretive frameworks. Radical Agnosis extends examination to the epistemic primitives through which frameworks and their apparent limits are articulated. Radical Dogmatism names the exemption from examination at either point.

For example, "The car is red" may remain warranted within an apparent shared reality. Treating that appearance as establishing the absolute ontological standing of redness, sensory access, or interpretation makes a further claim. The relative determination and the claim to metaphysical finality remain distinct.

An established tradition or a successful method may contribute reasons for trust. Neither establishes that its interpretations are incapable of error.

## Trust and Correction

Confidence, trust, strong attachment, stubbornness, and discomfort with correction do not establish Radical Dogmatism. The diagnostic distinction is whether possible error and warranted correction remain admissible.

A person may find a framework explanatory, argue for it firmly, and dislike being mistaken while acknowledging that the position could be wrong. Openness to correction does not require enjoyment of correction or acceptance of an unsupported declaration that a position is wrong.

The distinction concerns claimed infallibility, not the intensity of conviction or the emotional ease of reconsideration.

## Certainty-Uncertainty and Self-Application

Certainty and uncertainty remain distinct relative epistemic modes. Radical Dogmatism names neither certainty itself nor a necessary counterpart to uncertainty.

Its diagnostic scope includes negative finality-claims. Declaring "It is absolutely certain that nothing is absolutely certain" exempts a claim about uncertainty from the examination it directs toward certainty.

Radical Skepticism and Radical Agnosis retain their self-application: their formulations remain open to examination. Gnostic-Agnosticism preserves experiential participation without finalized interpretation.

The distinction is epistemic, not a hypostatic assignment. Neither certainty nor uncertainty is identified with a separate hypostatic principle, and their relation supplies no final characterization of what exceeds knowability.

## Terminological Scope

Doctrinal absolutism describes an application involving a doctrine. Radical Dogmatism also encompasses claims concerning sensory appearance, disciplinary method, and the apparent conditions of knowing, whether or not they are presented as doctrine.

"Radical" concerns the claimed basis of certainty. "Universal" would concern the breadth of a claim. A single domain may be declared infallible without a claim to knowledge of everything.

The definition stated here determines the Zenetist sense. A different philosophical definition attached to the word "dogmatism" does not alter this diagnostic distinction.
