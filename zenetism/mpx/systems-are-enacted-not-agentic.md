# Systems Are Enacted, Not Agentic
## On Human Agency, Institutional Abstraction, and the Meaning of "They"

**Function:** Distinguishes an enacted relational system from the persons and computational processes through which it becomes operative

## 1. The System Is Not an Actor

A system does not act.

What is called a system is an abstraction over relations enacted through actual beings, decisions, technologies, rules, institutions, incentives, habits, and repeated forms of participation.

The abstraction may be useful for describing the pattern.

It should not be mistaken for an independently acting entity.

A policy does not enforce itself.

An institution does not decide apart from the persons and processes through which decisions become operative.

An algorithmic arrangement does not appear from nowhere.

A distribution architecture does not choose its own criteria.

People establish, modify, maintain, apply, reproduce, defend, or abandon these relations.

Computational processes may execute portions of them, but those processes likewise arise within designed and maintained arrangements.

> **A system is enacted, not self-acting.**

---

## 2. Apparent Systemic Solidity

A long-maintained system can appear to possess an existence of its own.

Its rules precede the present participant.

Its institutions persist across generations.

Its effects may continue when individual participants change.

This persistence can make the abstraction appear more substantial than the beings continuously reproducing it.

But remove the enactment and the apparent independent existence disappears.

A law no one recognizes, applies, or enforces has no operative social existence.

An institution wholly abandoned ceases to function as an institution.

A hierarchy no one performs ceases to organize anyone.

What appeared permanent was sustained relation.

> **Persistence of enactment is not independent existence.**

> **When enactment ceases, the apparent solidity of the system is disclosed as relational rather than self-subsisting.**

---

## 3. The Meaning of "They"

Where the complete actor map is unavailable, **they** may lawfully refer to the actual but incompletely identified persons participating in the operative relation.

It does not imply:

- one mastermind
- one institution
- identical motives among every participant
- complete knowledge of the transmission chain
- or identification of every person involved

It means simply:

> **whoever the relevant actors are**

Those actors may occupy different positions within the same pattern.

Some may design.

Some may implement.

Some may maintain.

Some may apply.

Some may amplify.

Some may deny.

Some may reproduce a pattern without understanding its deeper relation.

Unknown identity does not make the actors unreal.

> **Unknown actor identity is not absence of actors.**

> **Distributed implementation is not actorlessness.**

---

## 4. Three Registers of Attribution

The following should remain distinct:

### Observed actors and artifacts

Persons, institutions, publications, outputs, statements, decisions, or technical events directly present within the available record.

### Diagnosed pattern

The larger operative relation disclosed through recurrence among those events.

A structural diagnosis does not require complete identification of every participant before the pattern itself can be recognized.

### Hypothesized larger cause

Claims concerning the fuller coordination, motive, initiating actors, or ultimate causal architecture behind the diagnosed pattern.

These may remain speculative even where the pattern itself is strongly diagnosed.

The existence of a speculative third register does not reduce the first two to speculation.

---

## 5. System-Language and Causal Displacement

Statements such as:

> "The system did this."

can obscure agency where they are treated literally.

A more precise formulation is:

> **Persons and computational processes acting through an institutional or technical arrangement produced the outcome.**

System-language remains useful as shorthand for the arrangement.

It should not become a means of evacuating agents from the causal account.

This matters especially where institutional effects are harmful.

To say that an outcome was "systemic" must not silently become:

> "Therefore nobody did it."

The two propositions are unrelated.

A distributed pattern may involve many actors rather than none.

---

## 6. Relation to Structural Diagnosis

Structural diagnosis concerns the operative relation beneath its changing embodied participants.

The relation may recur while the persons carrying it change.

This does not make the relation a phantom actor.

It means that differentiated persons may repeatedly instantiate the same structural orientation.

The pattern is real as relation.

The actors are real as bearers and enactors.

Neither should be substituted for the other.

> **The pattern does not act instead of its bearers.**

> **Its bearers enact the pattern through differentiated manifestations.**

---

## 7. Standing Distinctions

> **systemic does not mean actorless**

> **abstraction is not agent**

> **unknown actors are not nonexistent actors**

> **distributed causation is not causation without persons**

> **persistence of enactment is not independent existence**

> **a system is enacted, not self-acting**

> **the pattern does not replace the beings through which it becomes operative**

---

## Closing Principle

A system appears to act only because its enactment is distributed widely enough that the individual relations producing it become difficult to see.

Remove the beings, decisions, practices, technologies, and repeated participation through which it is enacted, and no independent actor called "the system" remains.

The abstraction describes the pattern.

The beings make it operative.
