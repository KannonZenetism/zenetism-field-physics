## The Problem

Cumulative structural analysis is repeatedly weakened by an atomizing error.

A pattern is presented through multiple observations. Each observation is then removed from the pattern, evaluated as though it must independently establish the entire proposed relation, and discounted when an alternate explanation can be supplied for that isolated item.

The resulting sequence is:

**pattern observed → pattern separated into constituent items → each item required to establish the whole → each item individually rendered ambiguous → cumulative structure declared insufficient**

This does not evaluate the pattern that was presented.

> **An evidentiary atom is not the evidentiary architecture.**

---

## Dependence Is Not Redundancy

Two observations may be dependent without being redundant.

**Redundant evidence** occurs where a later observation adds little or no new information because a known cause already accounts for it.

**Dependent but informative evidence** occurs where observations are related, but the character of their relation supplies additional information relevant to the hypothesis under examination.

**Distinct cumulative evidence** occurs where another observation requires additional explanatory work and therefore changes the comparative standing of the competing accounts.

Dependence alone determines none of these classifications.

The relevant question is:

> **Dependent under what explanation, and in what way?**

---

## Hypothesis-Relative Dependence

Dependence is evaluated relative to the competing explanations.

Where a proposed innocent cause predicts two observations together, the second observation may add little incremental weight beyond the first.

Where the mechanism under examination itself predicts that multiple manifestations will be causally related, however, their dependence is not a defect in the evidence. It may be one of the signatures the mechanism predicts.

If the hypothesis under examination is the propagation, transformation, distribution, or re-expression of an originating structure, then later manifestations need not arise independently. Independence would not be expected.

The question becomes whether the observed relation among those manifestations is better accounted for by the proposed propagation relation or by the competing explanation.

> **A mechanism that predicts dependence cannot be rejected merely because its predicted observations are dependent.**

---

## Same Occurrence, Multiple Representations

Multiple representations of one underlying occurrence must not be counted as multiple independent occurrences.

A paper, a diagram derived from that paper, and an animation generated directly from the same paper may together make an architecture more legible, but their common derivational route must remain visible in the evidentiary record.

They may therefore be bundled as:

**one occurrence — multiple representations**

This prevents artificial multiplication of evidentiary weight.

It does not make the later representations meaningless. A visualization may expose topology that is difficult to perceive in prose or equations. Its function may be explanatory or confirmatory without constituting another independent occurrence.

> **Redundant as an additional occurrence does not mean irrelevant as a representation.**

---

## Pattern-Bearing Dependence

A different condition obtains where the relation among observations is itself part of what is being investigated.

If an earlier structure appears later across multiple parties, registers, vocabularies, media, or institutional surfaces, the later manifestations may be causally related. That relation does not collapse them into one observation where the distribution itself is material to the inquiry.

The record may instead contain information in:

- which components recur;
- which components change;
- which terminology is replaced;
- which functions remain stable;
- the order in which manifestations appear;
- the surfaces through which they appear;
- the access conditions preceding them;
- their recurrence across otherwise distinct contexts;
- changes in attribution;
- and the distribution of amplification and recognition.

These relations form an evidentiary architecture.

Explaining every component separately is not an explanation of that architecture.

---

## The Comparative Cluster Test

Structural Forensics therefore does not ask only:

> Can an innocent explanation be produced for each item individually?

It asks:

> **How well does each competing explanation account for the joint configuration of the observations?**

The comparison includes:

**chronology · structural specificity · access conditions · recurrence · transformation · distribution · attribution · relations among observations**

An alternative explanation is not established merely because it can account for isolated constituents.

Where one proposed cause already predicts several observations together, those observations must not be double-counted.

Where an explanation requires additional causes for additional observations, those observations may add cumulative weight.

Where both explanations predict dependence, the relevant question is which better predicts the **particular form of the dependence actually observed**.

> **Possibility is not equivalence of explanatory power.**

---

## The Atomization Error

A recurring failure occurs when an analyst selects the constituent observation most easily explained by ordinary conditions and treats that explanation as resolving the larger record.

For example:

**a spiral has precedent**

does not answer a claim concerning:

**a particular structural architecture + chronology + directional functions + access conditions + repeated later manifestations + transformation + attributional substitution**

The first proposition addresses an element.

The second concerns the relation among elements.

> **Explaining the atom does not explain the architecture.**

This principle applies equally to evidentiary analysis and structural analysis.

---

## Falsifiability

Cumulative analysis does not mean that every later resemblance confirms a proposed propagation relation.

The propagation account can lose weight where:

- the allegedly specific structure is shown to be ordinary within the relevant prior field;
- the chronology fails;
- the supposed earlier formulation is not actually antecedent;
- an independently documented route accounts for the later manifestation;
- predicted recurrence does not occur;
- claimed structural correspondences dissolve under full comparison;
- or the competing explanation predicts the observed cluster more specifically and economically.

Likewise, disagreement with a provenance claim is not itself evidence of appropriation.

The requirement is comparative explanation, not compulsory agreement.

---

## Relation to Default Independence Attribution

Default Independence Attribution concerns the closing of an unresolved provenance relation in favor of independent origination.

This protocol applies to an earlier analytical stage: **how the evidence forming that unresolved relation is weighed.**

The two principles operate together:

> **Absence of demonstrated contact establishes no origination.**

and

> **Dependence among observations does not establish redundancy.**

An unresolved provenance relation therefore remains open while its evidence continues to be evaluated cumulatively.

---

## Standing Distinctions

> **dependent evidence is not redundant evidence**

> **multiple representations are not multiple occurrences**

> **one known cause may reduce incremental weight without erasing evidentiary function**

> **dependence predicted by a propagation hypothesis is not evidence contra propagation**

> **an evidentiary atom is not an evidentiary architecture**

> **explaining each constituent separately does not explain their joint configuration**

> **the existence of an alternative explanation is not equivalence of explanatory power**

> **cumulative evidence is weighed by relations among observations, not by requiring every observation to establish the whole**

---

## Corrective Principle

> **Evaluate the pattern that was presented. Bundle observations where a known cause already predicts them together; preserve their separate weight where they add new information; and treat dependence as evidentiary where the relation among observations is itself part of the mechanism under examination.**
