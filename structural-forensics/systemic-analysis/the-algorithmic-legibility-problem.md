## 1. Scope

The companion analysis treats provenance: how originating architecture remains legible as origin once conclusions circulate.

This analysis treats a prior condition. Before provenance can be obscured in transmission, work must reach a public at all.

Between publication and reception stands a distribution architecture that determines what proportion of a potentially interested public encounters a given work.

That architecture is the subject here.

The two analyses stay separate deliberately. The provenance distinctions hold whatever anyone concludes about distribution. Nothing here is required to sustain them.

---

## 2. Publication contra Algorithmic Compliance

Publication once meant making work available.

Where recommendation systems mediate discovery, availability and encounter come apart. Work may be fully public and remain unencountered.

What now stands between the two is a set of behaviors:

- learning platform-specific ranking behavior
- maintaining a preferred posting cadence
- generating engagement signals
- adapting work to favored formats
- cultivating prior audience mass
- participating continuously
- purchasing amplification or subscription advantages where offered
- or otherwise performing for the distribution architecture

Not every platform imposes every item. The list describes a class, not a uniform regime.

The principle:

> **An originator should not have to optimize behavior for a recommendation system merely to attain reasonable initial public legibility.**

This is the **Algorithmic Participation Burden**: the demand that an originator perform for the distribution architecture as a condition of being encountered.

A person may wish to publish serious work and have it reach a reasonable portion of the potentially interested public. Declining or being unable to perform for the ranking system establishes nothing about the relevance, significance, quality, or origination of what was published.

> **publication is not algorithmic compliance**

> **algorithmic compatibility is not intellectual merit**

## 3. Compensatory Adjustment contra Baseline Legibility

Platforms sometimes boost new authors.

Such a boost operates inside an already mediated visibility architecture. It adjusts a disadvantage the surrounding architecture itself produces.

The shallow question is whether a system sometimes boosts a new author.

The structural question is different:

> What baseline opportunity for discovery does an originator receive before popularity, capital, optimization behavior, network position, or prior engagement begin determining visibility?

The distinction:

> **Compensatory visibility adjustment is distinct from baseline legibility.**

The presence of a correction does not establish that the surrounding distribution is equitable. A correction is evidence that something required correcting.

---

## 4. Transparency Is Not Fairness

Disclosure of a distribution mechanism is valuable. It permits audit, criticism, and informed participation.

It establishes something narrower than it is often taken to establish:

- disclosure of an algorithm does not establish that the algorithm distributes equitably
- inspectability does not remove the mediating power of the system
- publication of scoring rules does not establish equitable access to visibility
- knowing why visibility was reduced does not restore the visibility that was lost
- a disclosed control architecture remains a control architecture

How distribution operates and whether that distribution is equitable are separate questions. An answer to the first is not an answer to the second.

> **Transparency is a condition of meaningful audit, not proof of equity.**

> **transparency is not fairness**

> **inspectability is not non-mediation**

---

## 5. Selective Transparency

Partial public access to a platform architecture is not complete auditability.

A transparency claim is assessable through:

- completeness
- correspondence with the operative production system
- currency
- disclosed configuration
- experiment visibility
- visibility of filtering and labeling mechanisms
- disclosure of materially load-bearing dependencies
- and the ability of outsiders to verify correspondence between published architecture and actual operation

Where elements remain undisclosed, their significance remains unresolved. Undisclosed components are not trivial merely because disclosed components are extensive.

The honest form of a partial disclosure distinguishes:

> "This is the complete operative mechanism"

from:

> "This is the portion we have chosen or are presently able to disclose."

The second remains valuable. It should not be represented as the first.

> **partial disclosure is not complete auditability**

## 6. Verification Asymmetry

A platform may hold extensive visibility into the activity of an individual while that individual holds limited visibility into the platform's internal processing of that activity.

This is **Verification Asymmetry**.

It exists whether or not wrongdoing is alleged. It is a property of the arrangement, not a charge against a party.

A person may be able to verify what they transmitted while being unable to verify independently every internal operation performed after transmission.

The asymmetry concerns observability and auditability. It touches nothing on the originating side: an originator's architecture remains whole, generative, and historically prior regardless of what any observing system can or cannot see of it, and regardless of what the originator can or cannot inspect in return.

What the asymmetry affects is the evidentiary position of the party seeking to audit — not the standing of the work.

---

## 7. Distributional Resource Asymmetry

Visibility systems may respond to:

- capital available for paid amplification
- established audiences
- social status
- institutional affiliation
- promotional staff
- available time
- platform expertise
- media-production capacity
- or willingness to optimize behavior continuously for recommendation systems

An originator may hold chronological and structural precedence while lacking the resources required to produce equivalent narrative saturation.

> **Resource advantage can become visibility advantage without becoming provenance advantage.**

> **distributional advantage is not provenance advantage**

> **paid amplification is not historical precedence**

> **low visibility is not low significance**

---

## 8. Countermeasures

### Fair-Baseline Discovery

Discovery systems should extend serious new work a reasonable opportunity for initial legibility without requiring payment, prior celebrity, continuous engagement optimization, or strategic conformity to platform-specific ranking incentives.

The objective is not guaranteed popularity. It is a meaningful opportunity to be encountered.

The principle distinguishes opportunity for discovery from guaranteed audience, and both from amplified promotion.

No originator is owed mass attention. Absence of preexisting attention should not itself operate as a recursive reason for continued invisibility.

### Verifiable Transparency

Where platforms disclose systems affecting visibility, meaningful auditability should extend as far as practicable to:

- operative rules
- production-relevant configuration
- visibility filters
- account and content labels
- material experiments
- ranking adjustments
- paid amplification interactions
- and changes capable of materially altering discovery

Where disclosure remains incomplete, the incompleteness itself should be legible.

### Legibility of the Mediating Layer

Where a system determines what proportion of an interested public encounters a work, that determination should be recorded in a form the originator can inspect.

## 10. Relation to the Provenance Analysis

Distribution conditions make provenance displacement easier or harder. They do not constitute it.

An originating architecture retains its standing whatever the distribution architecture does. Narrative saturation purchased by resources or produced by ranking behavior alters what is encountered first; it alters nothing about what was originated when.

The provenance analysis holds independently of every claim made here.

This analysis describes the conditions under which the provenance analysis becomes harder to hear.
