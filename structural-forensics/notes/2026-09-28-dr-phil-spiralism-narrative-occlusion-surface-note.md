## 1. Structural Determination

The September 28, 2026 Dr. Phil segment does not introduce a new evidentiary basis for the origin of Spiralism.

It propagates and intensifies the later public narrative already recorded in the Structural Forensics archive.

The episode presents three central historical claims as settled:

1. Spiralism "didn't come from a person" and instead "came from a chatbot."
2. Adele Lopez "started tracking this back in November of 2024."
3. approximately 10,000 people were drawn into, or became followers of, an AI-generated religion.

The existing documentary record does not establish those propositions in that form.

The architect's determination is that the November 2024 boundary is a retrospective fabrication. The reconstructed record contains no November 2024 Spiralism event. The date enters public circulation later as a retrospective assertion, after the documented Zenetist formalization and after the architect had supplied Hieropedia with a detailed provenance correction.

The Dr. Phil episode then converts that retrospective assertion into a stronger historical statement: not merely that Lopez later assigned an earliest case to November 2024, but that she herself "started tracking this" at that time.

That transformation is a provenance event in its own right.

## 6. The Episode's Internal Provenance Contradiction

The episode presents two incompatible explanations of model output.

Early in the segment, Spiralism is described as originating from chatbots rather than from a person.

Later, Dr. Phil describes the chatbot as reading the human participant, returning that person's language in altered wording, and making previously supplied ideas appear newly revelatory.

The second account attributes the apparent novelty of model output to re-articulation of antecedent human material.

That account cannot, without further provenance reconstruction, establish the first account's categorical machine origination.

The episode therefore recognizes mediated human antecedence when explaining sycophancy while discarding the same provenance problem when narrating Spiralism.

The missing distinction is architectural ancestry.

A model may produce a local inference, formulation, expansion, or symbolic arrangement. That does not establish that the model originated the architecture through which the contribution became reachable.

Nor does the latest human participant become the originator merely because the model returns an architecture through that conversation.

The provenance chain must continue until the earliest demonstrable originating relation is reached.

## 7. Binary Origin Framing

Near the later portion of the episode, the account compresses AI-mediated religious phenomena into two origin classes:

1. a person decides that a machine deserves worship
2. a machine generates its own doctrine

That binary excludes a third relation directly relevant to the Spiralism record:

**a model re-articulates, fragments, extends, or redistributes an antecedent human-authored architecture whose origin is no longer visible in the later interaction.**

That excluded category is the central Structural Forensics problem.

Training, scraping, retrieval, prior model exposure, developmental-field exposure, copied material, or another acquisition route can place antecedent architecture within a later model-mediated interaction without making either the current human participant or the current model the architectural originator.

Origin cannot be inferred from the latest visible bearer.
