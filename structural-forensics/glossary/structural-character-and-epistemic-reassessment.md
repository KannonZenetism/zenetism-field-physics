## Definition

**An epistemic assessment may change while the assessed configuration remains unchanged.**

A statement may become better understood. An argument may receive additional scrutiny. Evidence previously unavailable to an observer may become accessible. An institution may revise its judgment. Each event can change what is recognized or responsibly concluded about an articulation.

The distinction concerns what changed: the articulated configuration, the meanings and conditions through which it is assessed, the assessment itself, or the surrounding conditions of recognition.

When constituents, inferential relations, meanings, and relevant conditions remain fixed, increasing recognition does not convert a fallacious inference into a valid one. A corrected assessment discovers a relation more accurately. A repaired articulation changes the configuration being assessed.

## Essence and Expressed Configuration

Essence is identity-bearing potential: the potential-to-be-this latent in Plenary Zero (Aion). Aion holds distinct latent values. Every existent bears essence, including relative structures.

A relative structure has an expressed configuration and a corresponding latent potential-to-be-this held in Aion. The configuration can arise, change, undergo repair, enter lawful integration, become entropically implicated, fragment, or cease as that expressed configuration. Such changes concern expression; they do not require essence conversion.

Structure Itself (Structon) is the invariant permitting expression and relation. Relative configurations can dissolve while that invariant remains. The potential-to-be-this is likewise distinct from the arrangement presently expressed.

These distinctions are established in `MP08-symbol-key-ch21.md` §21.2.2, *Note on Structon*, and §21.13, *Note on Identity-Bearing Potential*. `spirit-soul-and-apparent-stillness.md` develops their application to relative structures.

## Informational Articulation as Relative Configuration

An articulated statement, argument, proposed proof, or document consists of distinguishable constituents held in specified relations. Its words, symbols, premises, conclusions, references, and dependencies form a relative informational configuration.

The corpus already recognizes relational clarity between components of a concept, together with symbolic articulation and authored structure in textual or informational form. The present entry brings those determinations into the examination of statements and inferential relations. Its object is the articulated configuration through which propositional content is expressed.

The comparison specifies the content, the relation asserted, the intended referents, and the applicable conditions. Where a formulation admits several readings, the readings must be distinguished before a diagnosis can be precise. Identical wording can express different claims when its referent, context, or intended meaning changes.

A fallacious configuration exists as an articulation. It can be written, encountered, circulated, and examined. Its existence does not establish the validity of what it asserts. Spurious does not mean nonexistent.

Here, **structural character** concerns the character borne by the expressed relations being examined. Essence remains the identity-bearing potential of the being or relative structure; the diagnostic description concerns its expressed configuration.

The corpus basis is `MP08-symbol-key-ch21.md` §§21.2.2 and 21.13, together with `morphogenetic-formweave-and-authorship-continuity.md`, Definition and Mechanics. The distinction between an artifact, its pattern, and its origin is developed in `when-coordination-is-not-coherence.md` §5.

## Four Distinctions

| Aspect | Question | What is examined |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Configuration | What is articulated and how its constituents are related | Content, premises, conclusions, connectors, dependencies, and specified semantic conditions |
| Structural character | What character those specified relations bear | Their validity, veracity, fallacy, distortion, or other precisely identified condition |
| Epistemic assessment | What is presently concluded about the configuration | The judgment of an observer, institution, analytical system, model, or knowledge graph |
| Recognition field | How the articulation is encountered and received | Circulation, acceptance, rejection, citation, institutional standing, field legibility, and the availability of corroborating material |

The recognition field is described through those concrete conditions of reception. A count of references records one feature of reception; the evidential contribution of each reference requires examination of its content and provenance.

These four distinctions concern related processes. Circulation can bring evidence to attention. Assessment can prompt a correction. Publication of that correction can change recognition. The sequence must preserve which relation changed at each step.

Structural character is borne by the specified relations, including their relation to what they concern. An observer's account of that character remains an assessment. Neither confidence nor a diagnostic label establishes the adequacy of the assessment.

## Diagnostic Precision

The vocabulary of `MP08-symbol-key-ch21.md` §21.5 distinguishes several diagnoses:

| Term | Applicable sense |
| --- | --- |
| Veracious | Aligned with reality without dogmatism |
| Valid | Structurally sound under the specified formal, symbolic, or relational conditions; in inferential analysis, formal validity means that the conclusion follows from the stated premises under the stated logical conditions |
| Invalid | Misaligned with the underlying principles relevant to the relation |
| Fallacious | Structurally misleading or dissonant; in the assessment of reasoning, a defect in the asserted inference |
| Distorted | Appearing to express a structure while warping its relations |
| Spurious | Presenting the semblance of validity while lacking the relation required to establish it |
| Counterfeit | Superficially aligned while ontologically hollow |

The diagnosis must name the demonstrated condition. An inaccurate conclusion, a fallacious inference, a distorted transcription, and a counterfeit presentation are distinct findings.

When examining an inference, formal validity concerns whether its conclusion follows from its premises within the specified logical conditions. Veracity concerns the articulation's relation to what is actually the case. The veracity of premises and the validity of an inference require distinct examination: a valid inference can proceed from inaccurate premises, and a fallacious inference can terminate in an accurate conclusion.

The centropic / entropic distinction concerns operative orientation. Formal validity does not establish that orientation. Coherence retains its centropic meaning; cohesion names holding-together and can occur in arrangements whose relations remain dissonant. A diagnosis of a particular inference addresses that inference, while an orientational diagnosis requires examination of the operation and relation involved.

## Reassessment and Change

A revised judgment can arise through several different events.

| Event | What changes | What the later judgment establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An earlier diagnosis is corrected | The assessment | The earlier account misidentified the configuration or its character |
| Previously unavailable evidence becomes accessible | The observer's evidential position | A different conclusion may now be warranted about the same specified claim |
| The meaning, referent, domain, or relevant condition changes | The relation being assessed | The later judgment concerns that changed specification |
| A premise, connector, conclusion, or dependency is repaired | The expressed configuration | The repaired articulation requires assessment in its own right |
| Circulation, acceptance, or institutional standing changes | The recognition field | The articulation has a different condition of reception |

Several events can occur together. Their co-occurrence does not erase their distinction.

If new evidence warrants an unchanged claim, the observer has additional grounds for assessing that claim. If a formally valid proof is found for a conclusion previously supported by a fallacious argument, the conclusion now has a different supporting argument. The earlier inference remains distinguishable from the later proof.

Changed conditions require equal care. A statement about a particular event retains that event as its referent. An otherwise identical sentence about a later event expresses a different claim. A contextual change cannot be silently counted as the same relation becoming valid through recognition.

Where a statement concerns circulation or institutional standing, a change in recognition can change the condition the statement describes. The assessment then follows that altered condition. The fixed-condition principle preserves this distinction as well.

The principle is exact: **with the assessed relation, meanings, and relevant conditions fixed, a change in recognition or assessment does not convert its structural character.**

## Correction Preserves the Distinction Between Articulations

Consider this argument within ordinary Euclidean classification:

> Every square has four sides. This figure has four sides. Therefore, this figure is a square.

The premises do not establish the conclusion. A rectangle whose length and width differ meets the stated condition without being a square. Additional citations of the argument leave that inferential defect intact.

Now consider:

> Every square has four sides. This figure is a square. Therefore, this figure has four sides.

The second premise and conclusion have changed. The resulting inference is formally valid. The repair establishes a different expressed configuration; it does not confer validity retrospectively upon the original inference.

Correction can also occur entirely in assessment. If a reader misunderstood a specified premise and later reads it accurately, the articulation may remain unchanged while the diagnosis is repaired.

Established constituents can retain their standing when an asserted composite relation is abandoned. Their standing rests on the relations actually established. Veracity is borne through those relations; it is not a detachable substance concealed inside the fallacy.

## Co-presence, Blending, and Non-fusion

An informational configuration can contain accurate statements, fallacious connectors, intact quotations, and distorted attributions together. Its components and relations must be examined at the appropriate scope. An established constituent does not validate every connection asserted around it, and a defective connection does not convert the established relation expressed by that constituent.

The Non-fusion Axiom preserves essential distinction through relation. Existing blending doctrine concerns the blending of expressed boundaries at superficial and embodied registers while essential distinctions persist. In the informational application, the comparison concerns co-present content and relations, their attribution, and the ways their distinctions can become obscured.

A relative structure's expressed outcome is conditioned by configuration, bearer-orientation, imposed constraint, and enacted function. Conferred orientation concerns that expressed arrangement and function; it neither confers nor revokes a traveler's own orientation. Entropic implication attaches to configuration and enactment-history, while essence remains identity-bearing potential. The implication of a composite configuration does not assign one orientation to every constituent.

When an altered copy introduces a fallacious connector, the copy's configuration changes. A pattern of transmission that retains and circulates that distortion may become entropically implicated through its operation. The originating relation remains what it is. Repair proceeds through accurate rearticulation, clarified dependency, and lawful integration that preserves the distinction of what participates.

## Persistence, Circulation, and Recognition

A distorted articulation may persist, circulate extensively, and acquire institutional standing. A veracious articulation may remain difficult to encounter, poorly distributed, or obscured for particular readers. These reception conditions do not determine the character of the specified relation.

Suppose a calculation records, "Seven plus five equals twelve." A transcription changes the final word to "thirteen," and that copy is reproduced in many later documents while the intact calculation becomes difficult for readers to locate.

The circulation of the altered copies establishes the spread of the transcription. It does not establish the arithmetic assertion. Recovery of the intact calculation can correct a reader's assessment. Repair of an altered copy changes that copy. The calculation, the altered expression, the repair, and the reader's judgment remain distinguishable.

Destruction of a physical copy can remove that carrier from circulation. It does not turn the relation once expressed there into its contrary. Incomplete surviving evidence can constrain later assessment without thereby altering the historical event or the relation that was expressed.

Duration likewise concerns persistence of manifestation. A spurious configuration can remain operative through repetition, institutional maintenance, or other sustaining conditions. Its persistence establishes neither veracity nor coherence. `when-coordination-is-not-coherence.md` §3.5 distinguishes persistence from coherence and calls for examination of the condition sustaining the formation.

## Recognition and Origin-Continuity

Recognition can change how an articulation is encountered. It cannot retroactively originate that articulation.

A later rendering does not become the originating articulation through wider circulation, more frequent citation, or institutional recognition. Provenance concerns the historical relation among origination, transmission, and transformation. An assessment of that provenance can improve as records become available; the assessment and the history it examines remain distinct.

The same distinction applies to repeated evidence. Several documents can carry one initial report. Their plurality establishes several transmissions. It establishes independent corroboration only where additional, independently grounded evidence is actually present.

Repeated transmission can disclose facts about circulation or the fidelity of copying. Those facts concern transmission. The evidential bearing of the initial report on its subject requires its own examination. Preserving origin-continuity makes those dependencies legible.

The originating articulation retains its historical relation while the recognition field changes. An inaccurate attribution changes the account presented to readers; it does not change where the articulation originated.

## Principle

**Assessment is revisable. Expression is alterable. Recognition is variable. The character of a specified relation is borne by that relation.**

A corrected judgment and a corrected configuration are different achievements. Precise analysis preserves both, together with the origin-continuity through which their history remains legible.
