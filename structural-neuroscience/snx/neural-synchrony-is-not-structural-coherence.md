## Abstract

Neural synchrony names selected relations among measured activities. Structural Coherence names a centropic relation preserving sovereign distinction and orientation. Their comparison requires the constituents, interactions, physical scale, observation, and functional continuation to remain explicit. Anatomical connectivity, statistical dependence, phase order, pairwise phase locking, and spectral coherence retain different meanings.

This extension examines how differentiated participation can support encompassing function while a composite statistic omits decisive relations. An exact three-constituent construction has equal present phase order and opposite instantaneous changes within one fixed dynamical system. The result proves a failure of autonomous prediction from that aggregate. It assigns no native orientation to either configuration. Evidence about attention, focal seizures, network integration, signal mixing, metastability, and criticality then establishes the measurement questions that a proposed neural realization must answer. Physical coordination matters; its functional interpretation requires more than a magnitude.

## 2. What a Network Measurement Describes

### 2.1 Distinct Relations

A neural network record first states what its nodes and edges represent. Nodes might denote tracked cells, cortical regions, recording contacts, or estimated components. Those choices do not preserve the same constituents. An edge might denote anatomy, a fitted influence, or a statistical dependence. The edge's mathematical appearance does not settle its physical meaning.

| Relation | Definition for the present investigation | Information not established by the relation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anatomical connectivity | A specified physical connection or pathway | Its present efficacy, timing, task contribution, or direction of information transfer |
| Functional connectivity | A declared statistical dependence among recorded activities | Direct interaction, a unique mechanism, or absence of common input |
| Phase synchronization | A specified relation among justified phase coordinates | Amplitude, metabolic support, tissue integrity, or task success |
| Global phase order | Magnitude of the mean unit phasor across selected constituents | The full phase arrangement or its assignment to constituent properties |
| Pairwise phase locking | Persistence of a phase difference across a declared interval | Direct coupling or successful encompassing function |
| Spectral coherence | Normalized cross-spectral dependence at a stated frequency | An anatomical edge or native Structural Coherence |

These definitions organize the proposed measurement record. Each application still needs acquisition conditions, preprocessing, an estimator, uncertainty, and a comparison interval. A physical phase coordinate also needs an oscillatory relation that supports it; assigning phases to an arbitrary signal does not establish the adequacy of a phase model.

### 2.2 Two Explicit Observations

For \(N\) constituent phases \(\theta_j\), global phase order is

\[
R(t)=\left|\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{i\theta_j(t)}\right|,
\qquad 0\le R\le1.
\]

The triangle inequality gives the interval. \(R=1\) requires the unit phasors to agree, while \(R=0\) requires their vector sum to vanish. The latter condition permits many organized arrangements.

For a fixed observation duration \(T>0\), define a pairwise phase-locking statistic

\[
P_{ij}=\left|\frac1T\int_0^T
e^{i(\theta_i(t)-\theta_j(t))}\,dt\right|.
\]

It is dimensionless and lies in \([0,1]\). Constant phase difference gives \(P_{ij}=1\), regardless of that difference's value. For \(\theta_1(t)=\omega t\) and \(\theta_2(t)=\omega t+\pi\), global \(R=0\) throughout the interval while \(P_{12}=1\). This exact anti-phase example is a scientific phase relation: zero global order is compatible with persistent pairwise coordination. Neither statistic measures independence in every admitted configuration.

## 3. Differentiated Participation and Functional Consequence

Integration and segregation require a named relation. In the proposed physical record, integration concerns participation across identified components in an encompassing process. Segregation concerns differentiated local participation or comparatively distinct statistical groups. Neither word supplies an orientation assessment. A task may require distinguishable responses together with selective communication between them.

Bosman and colleagues reported attention-related selective synchronization between macaque visual areas. The measured relation was frequency-specific and input-specific. Its scope supports a question about selective participation, not a universal demand for maximal whole-brain synchrony. [N1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22958827/).

Shine and colleagues associated more integrated time-resolved fMRI network states with better performance in the examined task. The finding concerns the chosen graph measures and task conditions. It does not identify every kind of physical integration with every cognitive capacity. [N4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02976).

Pathological activity also requires constituent and temporal detail. Truccolo and colleagues found heterogeneous single-neuron activity during focal-seizure initiation and spread, with more homogeneous cessation near termination. A description of every seizure phase as uniform hypersynchrony would omit those recorded differences. [N2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21441925/).

These cases motivate three separate questions. Which participants coordinate? What local differences remain operative? What encompassing consequence occurs? A physical relationship can assist one process and impair another, or change its consequence across intervals. That assessment needs an independently specified outcome, such as retained discrimination, response reliability, or continued access to a declared task.

The native comparison concerns differentiated participants and their relation within that assessed function. It does not convert selective synchrony into a certificate of centropy or heterogeneous activity into a certificate of entropy. Native orientation includes relations absent from the selected neural statistic.

## 4. Observation, Information Retention, and Continuation

Let \(x\) denote the admitted physical state and \(\pi(x)\) its observation. An observation fiber consists of all admitted states with the same observed value. A measurement may be precise while leaving many physical states in one fiber. Precision of measurement and completeness of representation are distinct properties.

For a selected feature \(f\), a readout \(\hat f\) satisfying \(f=\hat f\circ\pi\) exists exactly when \(f\) is constant on each fiber. If two states share an observation but differ in \(f\), no function of that observation can return \(f\) for both. Conversely, fiber constancy permits a readout assigning each observed value its common feature value. This is the feature-recovery criterion in LM08 §7.1.

Prediction asks an additional question. For a discrete state transition \(F\), autonomous observed evolution requires

\[
\pi(x)=\pi(y)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\pi(F(x))=\pi(F(y)).
\]

For differentiable dynamics \(\dot x=V(x)\), an autonomous observed vector field requires

\[
D\pi(x)V(x)=D\pi(y)V(y)
\quad\text{whenever}\quad \pi(x)=\pi(y).
\]

These are closure conditions for the named observation. Smoothness of a reduced field requires additional regularity. A failure of the necessary equality condition is already enough to exclude the proposed autonomous reduction.

An empirical record can respond to such a failure by retaining more constituent information, past observations, external input, or a probabilistic conditional description. Those are different constructions with different claims. The failure does not imply that every prediction is impossible. It identifies information missing from one proposed state description.

## 5. Equal Synchrony and Opposite Instantaneous Change

### 5.1 One Fixed Dynamical System

Consider three persistent constituent labels with dimensionless phases on the three-dimensional torus. On a complete interaction graph, let

\[
\dot\theta_i=\omega_i+\frac{\kappa}{3}
\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i),
\qquad
(\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3)=(\Omega,0,0),
\qquad \Omega,\kappa>0.
\]

The \(j=i\) term vanishes. \(\Omega\) and \(\kappa\) have units of inverse time; the zero frequencies refer to a common rotating frame. The frequency assignment and coupling remain fixed. The smooth vector field on the compact torus has unique trajectories for every finite time.

This is a specified phase-oscillator construction. Its sinusoidal interaction is a modelling assumption. A proposed neural realization requires evidence for the specified frequencies, graph, and interaction law.

Define

\[
z=\frac13\sum_{j=1}^{3}e^{i\theta_j},
\qquad R=|z|,
\qquad Q=|z|^2.
\]

\(Q\) is dimensionless and smooth even where \(R\) vanishes. Compare two physical phase configurations:

\[
A=(0,\pi/2,-\pi/2),
\qquad
B=(\pi/2,0,-\pi/2).
\]

Both have the same phase multiset, \(z=1/3\), \(R=1/3\), and \(Q=1/9\). Their assignment of phases to the fixed frequency-bearing constituents differs. This is a change of initial state within one system; it is not a change of the graph or parameters.

### 5.2 Exact Calculation

Substitution into the interaction equation gives

\[
\dot\theta(A)=(\Omega,-\kappa/3,\kappa/3),
\qquad
\dot\theta(B)=(\Omega-\kappa/3,0,\kappa/3).
\]

The complex observation and its squared magnitude satisfy

\[
\dot z=\frac{i}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{3}
e^{i\theta_j}\dot\theta_j,
\qquad
\dot Q=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline z\dot z).
\]

Consequently,

\[
\dot z(A)=\frac{2\kappa}{9}+\frac{i\Omega}{3},
\qquad
\dot z(B)=\frac{2\kappa}{9}-\frac{\Omega}{3},
\]

and

\[
\dot Q(A)=\frac{4\kappa}{27},
\qquad
\dot Q(B)=\frac{4\kappa}{27}-\frac{2\Omega}{9}.
\]

For \(\Omega=\kappa\), the rates have opposite signs:

\[
\boxed{
\dot Q(A)=\frac{4\kappa}{27}>0,
\qquad
\dot Q(B)=-\frac{2\kappa}{27}<0.
}
\]

### 5.3 What the Result Establishes

An autonomous scalar equation \(\dot Q=g(Q)\) would assign one derivative to the shared present value \(Q=1/9\). The computed derivatives differ. Therefore no such equation describes every admitted state of this system. Since \(R>0\) at both configurations and \(\dot R=\dot Q/(2R)\), the same obstruction applies to \(R\). Even the shared complex observation \(z\) has different derivatives here.

The omitted relation is phase assignment to constituent frequency. A phase histogram also omits that assignment. Retaining a complete phase vector together with the specified frequencies restores the state information needed by this construction. This statement concerns a mathematical state description, not exhaustive access to a physical brain.

The example establishes that equal aggregate synchrony can accompany different continuation within a fixed interaction system. Assessment of native orientation retains its architectural criteria. Task performance, injury, and metabolic expenditure require their own physical variables, measurements, and functional relations.

## 6. Metastability: Process and Estimator

Metastability must name the dynamical relation intended: persistence near configurations, transitions among them, or another specified form of transient coordination. A numerical estimator then measures a selected feature of that process. The estimator and the process are different objects.

Deco and colleagues quantified metastability through temporal variation of global phase order in filtered BOLD observations and a connected oscillator model. Their phases concerned slow hemodynamic signals. The statistic is a trajectory summary at that scale, not a direct description of individual spike dynamics. [N5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5465179/).

For illustration, define the interval standard deviation

\[
\overline R_T=\frac1T\int_0^T R(t)\,dt,
\qquad
M_T=\left[\frac1T\int_0^T
(R(t)-\overline R_T)^2\,dt\right]^{1/2}.
\]

\(M_T\) is dimensionless. It reports variability of \(R\) within the chosen interval. It does not retain the ordering of all observed values, the full sequence of constituent configurations, or the interaction responsible for a transition. The permanently in-phase and permanently anti-phase two-constituent examples both have \(M_T=0\), although their phase arrangements differ. A constant statistic therefore requires its own interpretation.

Hancock and colleagues distinguish several metastability concepts. The inspected author preprint also examines limits of phase-order variability, including omitted anti-phase organization; the published review and preprint remain distinct reference versions. [N6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39663408/), [preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202307.1445).

The proposed native comparison concerns continuing adjustment while a declared functional relation persists. Demonstrating that comparison requires observations of the function, participants, and continuation, alongside the chosen metastability measure. A statistic of reconfiguration does not identify Structural Coherence or native frozen equilibrium.

## 7. Criticality and Quasicriticality

A criticality claim needs a specified model, parameters varied in assessing the regime, system size, driving conditions, and diagnostic relation. A fitted distributional exponent describes a statistic. Its interpretation as evidence for a dynamical regime requires examining other mechanisms capable of producing it.

Touboul and Destexhe constructed power-law and scaling behavior without criticality. Their result limits the sufficiency of those signatures; it does not reject every neural criticality model. [N7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08033).

Fosque and colleagues presented evidence for quasicritical dynamics along a Widom line in driven systems. The proposal concerns changing apparent exponents and approximate scaling relations, with stated model and data comparisons. It does not establish one operating condition for every neural system. [N8](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevLett.126.098101).

Hagemann and colleagues found neither a general focal shift toward instability nor systematic pre-seizure drift in their human single-neuron analysis. Recording region, sampling, scale, and interval constrain that conclusion. It complicates a universal seizure-transition narrative. [N9](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008773).

For Structural Neuroscience, responsiveness and continuation are physical questions to examine within these defined regimes. Neither a critical point nor a response maximum supplies the native definition of centropy. Likewise, a physical departure from a proposed regime does not classify essential orientation. The relevant comparison must retain the specific function and the evidence supporting its physical articulation.

## 8. Measurement Uncertainty and the Domain Record

Palva and colleagues showed that EEG / MEG coupling estimators insensitive to direct zero-lag mixing can still contain spurious connections near interacting regions. A correction for one mixing effect therefore leaves other localization questions. [N3](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/183941/).

This limitation matters before interpreting an edge as a physiological interaction. The investigation should distinguish a changed physical relation from a changed recording mixture or estimator. Repeating an analysis with another justified observation can test robustness, while direct physical and perturbational evidence addresses different aspects of the proposed interaction.

| Record item | Required statement |
| --- | --- |
| Native relation | Distinction, participation, operative access, or continuation being articulated |
| Physical participants | Tracked constituents and uncertainty in correspondence across recordings |
| Scale and clock | Preparation, region, frequency band, sampling, and assessment interval |
| Interaction | Anatomical, physiological, statistical, or fitted relation represented by each edge |
| Boundary | External drive, bodily support, unrecorded participants, and task conditions |
| Observation | Signal acquisition, phase construction, estimator, preprocessing, and uncertainty |
| Retained information | The feature measured or predicted by the observation |
| Omitted information | Constituent assignment, amplitude, delays, costs, and unrepresented functions |
| Continuation | The task or physical relation assessed through time |
| Fidelity | Agreement between specified predictions and independent observations |

The record follows LM08 §13 and SN12 §4. Its purpose is concrete: each proposed identity becomes a checkable relation with stated limits. A physically meaningful network description may remain incomplete for predicting a task, while a task-predictive statistic may leave its physical mechanism unresolved.

## 9. Empirical Evaluation and Companion Relations

A direct empirical question is whether matched aggregate coordination accompanies different later responses once constituent assignment, input, or physical condition varies. Such a study would not need to reproduce the three-oscillator equations. It would examine the broader proposed relation between retained state information and predictive continuation.

The comparison should define the outcome independently from the synchrony statistic. If the outcome is computed from the same statistic, apparent agreement may express that definition rather than an encompassing function. Repeated observations, separate prediction intervals, and specified perturbations can distinguish a stable association from a response relation. Measurement uncertainty must remain in the comparison, especially when selecting apparently matched observations.

A second question concerns differentiation: which local responses persist while the encompassing task remains available? Equal aggregate performance across conditions may accompany different constituent participation. Whether that difference matters for later continuation requires a further observation, such as a changed input, delayed response, or recovery interval. The relevant result is the specified functional consequence.

SN12 retains the brain / Soul / Mind relation and the account of access, injury, development, and restoration. The energy companion examines material support and expenditure. The stabilization companion supplies physical examples of stationary observations with continuing processes. The entropy companion distinguishes signal statistics and physical consequence from native orientation.

Neural synchrony is consequently a family of physical relations requiring exact definitions. Its magnitude can be informative within a specified record. Structural Coherence retains the native centropic relation, sovereign distinction, and orientation that an aggregate neural measurement does not encode.
