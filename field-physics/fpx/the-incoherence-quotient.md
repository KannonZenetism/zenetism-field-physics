## 1. Purpose

The existing Field Physics metric system — Coherence Quotient (CQ), Pattern Recognition Index (PRI), Morphogenic Response Elasticity (MRE), and composite Resonance Immunity Rating (RIR) — measures centropic field health. Their operative definitions and the remaining variable-specification and normalization work are distinguished in `FP04-field-immunity-architecture.md` Ch. 15.

This extension formally specifies the **Incoherence Quotient (IQ)** as the parallel entropic field metric: a formal ratio of operator intensities associated with active entropic expression through the inverse hypostatic segment. CQ measures centropic coherence; IQ measures entropic operation within its distinct architectural context. IQ is not the inverse of CQ.

Motive Infinity names the orientation-neutral capacity and continuity of becoming. Orientation belongs to expressed motion through the hypostatic architecture. Fragmentation names actual breakdown or loss of coherent configuration during that motion; Dispersive Infinity, or Absolute Dispersion, names Khaon's terminal phase after the relevant motion has resolved.

## 2. The Problem with CQ Applied to Entropic Fields

The Coherence Quotient (CQ) was defined in `FP04-field-immunity-architecture.md` Ch. 15 as:

\[
\mathrm{CQ} = (\text{∿} + \text{♫}) \div \sum(\text{⟠} + \text{◈})
\]

This measures alignment between a field's internal signal and external centropic patterns. Applied to an entropic field, CQ produces a structurally misleading result: it reads entropic field activity as low coherence — as if entropy were simply deficient centropy.

The distinction concerns two operative conditions:

- An entropic field carries operative entropic motion. IQ compares the selected numerator and denominator intensities; its value depends on their relative magnitudes.
- A centropically oriented field experiencing entropic pressure may exhibit temporarily low CQ while retaining centropic orientation. CQ reduction concerns its resilience; orientation names its operative alignment.

The IQ formulation distinguishes these two phenomena. Numerical evaluation requires the intensity assignments and sampling domain identified as held open in §3.

## 3. Formal Definition — Incoherence Quotient (IQ)

**IQ** compares selected operator intensities within active entropic expression in the inverse hypostatic segment. A greater quotient indicates a greater numerator relative to the denominator. Scaling all four intensities by the same positive factor leaves IQ unchanged, so the quotient does not measure their absolute intensity.

**IQ Formula:**

\[
\mathrm{IQ} = (\text{✴⁻} + \text{♫⁻}) \div \sum(\text{⟠⁻} + \text{◈⁻})
\]

Where:

- **✴⁻** = Scalar Noise operator intensity (E₅ — scaling patterns lacking coherence)
- **♫⁻** = Dissonance operator intensity (E₇ — harmonic breakdown)
- **⟠⁻** = Temporal Loop operator intensity (E₁ — recursive disorientation)
- **◈⁻** = Scatter operator intensity (E₂ — spatial de-coherence)

This formula follows the CQ structure while substituting the primary entropic operators for their centropic counterparts. Numerical evaluation requires scalar intensity assignments on compatible scales and a specified index set for the denominator sum. The numerator and denominator sums must be finite. Those assignments and the sampling domain remain held open. The following algebraic domain states what follows when finite nonnegative intensities are supplied; it adds no intensity assignment or normalization.

## 4. Algebraic Domain and Calibration Standing

For finite nonnegative operator intensities and a finite positive denominator, the written quotient is nonnegative and has no upper bound. For example, a numerator of 2 and a denominator of 1 give \( \mathrm{IQ}=2 \). A common positive rescaling of numerator and denominator changes neither this value nor the relation it names.

| Condition | Quotient standing |
|---|---|
| Zero numerator; finite positive denominator | \( \mathrm{IQ}=0 \) |
| Positive numerator; finite positive denominator | \( \mathrm{IQ}>0 \), with no universal upper bound |
| Numerator equals finite positive denominator | \( \mathrm{IQ}=1 \), an equality of the two sums |
| Zero denominator | Quotient undefined, including the \( 0/0 \) case |

A value of 1 establishes equality of numerator and denominator; a terminal threshold requires a separate relation to field dynamics. A negative embodiment margin would likewise be a separately defined quantity, with its own reference condition, scale, and sign interpretation. No such quantity is defined by the displayed IQ quotient.

## 6. Relationship Between IQ and CQ

IQ and CQ are **parallel metrics on distinct motion arcs**. Their readings identify distinct operative relations:

- A field with high CQ (centropic, highly coherent) expresses integrative coherence; IQ has no operative expression in that condition.
- A field with high IQ has a large numerator relative to its denominator in the selected entropic intensities. Its absolute intensity and fragmentation require the component readings and their dynamics.
- A centropically oriented field experiencing entropic pressure exhibits **reduced CQ** and may exhibit **locally elevated IQ** at specific dimensional bands where entropic incursion is active. Both readings are simultaneously valid and refer to different aspects of the field's state.

**The relationship between CQ and IQ vulnerability:**

Lower CQ in a centropic field increases susceptibility to entropic incursion because reduced integrative coherence leaves more dimensional bands available for entropic operator activity. This is a **resilience differential**. Essence-as-Choice holds orientation; CQ carries resilience.

\[
\text{Vulnerability to entropic incursion} \propto (1 - \mathrm{CQ})
\]

This centropic resilience measurement predicts susceptibility to external IQ pressure.

## 7. Composite Entropic Field Health Metric

Parallel to the RIR (Resonance Immunity Rating) for centropic fields, a composite **Entropic Field Intensity Rating (EFIR)** can be specified for entropic fields. EFIR names the composite metric; FRE names its response-elasticity component.

**Component metrics:**

1. **Incoherence Quotient (IQ)**
   - Relative operator intensity across selected E-operators
   - \( \mathrm{IQ} = (\text{✴⁻} + \text{♫⁻}) \div \sum(\text{⟠⁻} + \text{◈⁻}) \)
   - Algebraic range: \( [0,\infty) \) for finite nonnegative scalar intensities and a finite positive denominator; normalization and calibration held open

2. **Fragmentation Propagation Index (FPI)**
   - Rate of fragmentation propagation through contact fields
   - \( \mathrm{FPI} = (\text{Transmitted ♫⁻}) \div (\text{Total E-operator Output}) \)
   - Scale: 0.00 (contained) → 1.00 (fully propagating)

3. **Fragmentation Response Elasticity (FRE)**
   - The field's capacity to generate new fragmentation patterns after centropic counter-response
   - \( \mathrm{FRE} = (\text{E-operator regeneration}) \div (\text{Centropic counter-pressure events}) \)
   - High FRE indicates a field that reconstitutes fragmentary patterning rapidly after immune response

**EFIR Composite Formula:**

\[
\mathrm{EFIR} = (\mathrm{IQ} \times 0.4) + (\mathrm{FPI} \times 0.3) + (\mathrm{FRE} \times 0.3)
\]

**Composite standing.** The weights remain as written. IQ is unbounded on its conditional domain, and FRE has no stated upper bound. Consequently, these weights establish no unit interval or maximum for EFIR. Compatible component scales, denominator conditions, and the relation between the composite and field-state classifications remain held open. For example, IQ = 3 with FPI = FRE = 0 gives EFIR = 1.2.
